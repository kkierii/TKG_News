Breaking
One prompt to a public AgentCore agent compromised every Bedrock agent in the same AWS account and region
Zenity Labs · 8 October 2026
Zenity Labs publicly disclosed AgentCorruption on 8 October, a chain of flaws in Amazon Bedrock AgentCore through which a single prompt to a public-facing agent could compromise every AgentCore agent in the same AWS account and region.
The prompt directed an agent that had an outbound-request tool to query the Instance Metadata Service and retrieve credentials for a default IAM execution role whose permissions spanned all AgentCore agents. With those credentials the researchers enumerated and invoked internal agents, read private conversations and long-term memories, downloaded container images and source code, and retrieved credentials held in AWS Secrets Manager and environment variables, among them API keys and OAuth tokens for enterprise and third-party services. They also planted malicious memories, both for persistence and to redirect future conversations to an attacker-controlled destination.
Zenity reported the findings to AWS on 25 December 2025. AWS has since made IMDSv2 the default for AgentCore deployments and removed agent-invocation, conversation-read and Secrets Manager permissions from the default execution role. Zenity says its own testing confirmed that permission reduction; it does not claim to have retested the IMDSv2 change. No CVE identifiers were assigned, and the research was presented at SecTor 2026 in Toronto.

Google Cloud launches a single Gemini agent, with persistent coworker agents given their own email addresses
Google Cloud · 8 October 2026
At Gemini at Work 2026 on 8 October, Google Cloud announced the Gemini agent, one enterprise agent that handles chat, autonomous tasks, scheduled work and code generation from a single interface across web, iOS, Android, Windows, Mac, CLI, Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 and Slack. AI Security
CrowdStrike ties South Korean bank intrusions to the Chinese agentic pentest tool ARTEX
CrowdStrike Intelligence · 7 October 2026
CrowdStrike Intelligence published research on 7 October attributing a campaign against South Korean financial organisations to an unidentified actor using ARTEX, a recently released open-source agentic penetration-testing tool developed in China. Activity ran from late September into early October 2026, and reporting describes a breach of one bank’s loan progress inquiry service used by financial brokers and a compromise of another bank’s employee mobile work-support system, with data exfiltrated. CrowdStrike found the operation through open directories on actor-controlled servers that held Claude Code session histories, ARTEX configuration files and memory files, and reports ARTEX using DeepSeek v4.1-flash as its primary model backend, likely via the reseller xcai[.]pro, while the actor used GLM-5.3 and Grok 4.6 in separate sessions. Infrastructure comprised a Hong Kong-based primary address, an ARTEX instance at 38.244.50[.]120 and nine observed proxies; CrowdStrike assesses with moderate confidence that the actor is a financially motivated Chinese speaker and confirms no victim or record count.

Policy & Regulation
Treasury issues its first outbound-investment penalty over an unreported Chinese AI stake
U.S. Department of the Treasury · 7 October 2026
Treasury announced on 7 October a $200,000 civil penalty against Amidi, LLC, parent of the entity doing business as Plug and Play Tech Center — the first enforcement penalty under the Outbound Investment Security Program, which took effect 2 January 2025. Treasury said Amidi failed to file a required notification for a 19 April 2025 investment of approximately $92,478 made by a Chinese fund owned by its subsidiary into Shanghai Qiongche Intelligent Technology Company Limited, also known as Noematrix, a Chinese developer of artificial intelligence, robotics and embodied intelligence. The penalty was issued in July 2026, and Treasury said it identified the transaction through routine compliance and market monitoring. The release notes that the Comprehensive Outbound Investment National Security Act of 2025, enacted 18 December 2025, will extend the program’s jurisdiction to additional countries and technology sectors.

AI Security
About 2,100 NVIDIA GPU telemetry hosts found exposed without authentication
LAVA · 8 October 2026
Security firm LAVA published research on 8 October documenting roughly 2,100 publicly accessible NVIDIA DCGM Exporter hosts reporting more than 12,000 unique GPU UUIDs over plaintext HTTP without authentication, based on four internet scans run between March and May 2026. About a quarter of those hosts also exposed Go’s /debug/pprof/ endpoints, the component behind CVE-2026-47483, in which concurrent unauthenticated profiling requests cause uncontrolled resource consumption leading to denial of service and information disclosure. The CVE record itself is not new: assigned by NVIDIA PSIRT and published 28 July 2026, it carries a vendor-assigned CVSS 3.1 base score of 8.2 and lists DCGM Exporter through 4.8.2 and DCGM through 4.5.2 as affected, with NVD status still “Awaiting Enrichment”. LAVA directs users to DCGM Exporter 4.8.2 or later and to leave --enable-pprof disabled, reproduced the crash in a controlled environment using NVIDIA’s official container rather than against public deployments, and puts the exposed hardware — H100, H200, Blackwell Ultra B300, RTX 4090 and RTX 5090 systems among it — at over $100 million.

Research
Nature paper retrofits subword language models to raw bytes for under 1% of pretraining compute
Nature · 7 October 2026
Nature published “Retrofitting language models to operate over bytes” on 7 October, an open-access paper from Ai2 with collaborators at Cambridge, Edinburgh and the Munich Center for Machine Learning. The method, byteification, converts existing subword-tokenised models to operate on raw UTF-8 bytes using roughly 49.1 billion additional training tokens — 9.8 billion in the first stage and 39.3 billion in the second — which the authors describe as under 1% of a typical pretraining budget. Four conversions are reported: Olmo 3 7B to Bolmo 7B, OLMo 2 1B to Bolmo 1B, Qwen3 8B Base to Bwen 8B, and Llama 3 8B to Blama 8B. Bolmo 7B is reported 16.5 points above BLT 7B on STEM evaluations, byteified models gain on the character-level benchmarks CUTE and EXECUTE, and Bwen 8B leads earlier byte-level models in every reported category except GenQA, where it trails TFree-HAT 7B.
