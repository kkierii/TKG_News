Breaking
Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia chief executive; CFO Colette Kress steps in as interim CEO
NVIDIA; CBS News · 12 November 2026
Jensen Huang resigned on Thursday as Nvidia’s president, chief executive and a board member, effective immediately, Nvidia announced. Chief financial officer Colette Kress becomes interim CEO while keeping her finance role, former Synopsys chief executive Harvey C. Jones becomes the board’s first-ever chair, and the board will consider internal and external candidates. Nvidia gave no reason; CBS News reported the exit followed an investigation into Huang’s relationship with Alice Huang, a Megaspeed International executive US intelligence suspects of spying for China, and that the shares closed down 4.3% at $166.19 on Thursday. Third-quarter results remain scheduled for 17 November.

Policy & Regulation
Lutnick agrees to testify as Bessent blames Commerce over Megaspeed chip exports
CBS News · 13 November 2026
Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick has agreed to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on 10 December, a US official told CBS News, as Republicans question why Singapore-based Megaspeed International was not added to the Entity List. The US government suspects Megaspeed, previously led by Alice Huang, of directing advanced Nvidia chips to China in breach of an export ban. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC on Friday that Treasury, War, the US Trade Representative and State “were in favor of putting Megaspeed on the Entity List for months, but Commerce has the pen.” The Commerce Department did not immediately respond to CBS News.

Nonprofits sue OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta and SpaceXSI to halt frontier AI development
CBS News; Reuters via Claims Journal · 13 November 2026
The Alliance for Secure AI, Irreplaceable Education Fund, Public Citizen and Heat Initiative sued five AI developers on Thursday in San Francisco Superior Court, CBS News reported. Citing California’s laws against unfair business practices, they seek a court order barring further development of the most advanced models, pointing to warnings from the companies’ own leaders about catastrophic risk, according to Reuters. The case follows state actions: Florida sued OpenAI in June and in September asked the judge to bar it from developing new models without outside oversight, a motion still pending, and eight states sued OpenAI in October.

Google releases Gemini 4 Hydrogen at $1.50 input and $7.50 output per million tokens
Google · 12–13 November 2026
Google released Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a faster model for coding and agent workflows that sits alongside its frontier Gemini 4 Carbon and Argon, priced flat across a 1 million-token context at $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens. Google says it finishes tasks 2.5 times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash at its default high thinking level and scores 57% on Terminal-Bench 4.1, against 18.2%. Google puts it at 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at high and 54 at a new xhigh setting, against 50 for Argon. It is rolling out in the Gemini API, AI Studio, Gemini for business, the Gemini app and Search.

Chips & Infrastructure
Analysis finds Blackwell fingerprints in leading Chinese open-weight models
TechCrunch · 13 November 2026
Will DePue, a former OpenAI researcher who leads the nonprofit Unemployment Labs, analyzed the open weights of models from DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax and Ant Group and found evidence that Moonshot’s Kimi K3 releases and DeepSeek’s V4 models bear fingerprints of training on Blackwell chips, which are banned from export to China, TechCrunch reported. TechCrunch notes that does not mean the models were trained entirely on Nvidia chips or that export laws were broken, since the chips could have been in offshore data centres. Epoch AI estimates about two million advanced chips entered China in 2024 and 2025 through schemes designed to hide their destination, and 275,000 Nvidia accelerators were imported to Malaysia.

AI Security
FBI says North Korean hackers stole more than $400 million using deepfake video calls
The Hacker News · 13 November 2026
The FBI says North Korea-linked hackers stole more than $400 million in cryptocurrency over the last 18 months through FaceStealer, a social-engineering scheme that uses fake video meetings, surreptitiously recorded videos and deepfakes. US authorities seized the campaign’s cloud infrastructure, and the FBI seized more than 900 victim webcam recordings used to impersonate victims in follow-on attacks. The activity is linked to the BlueNoroff cluster, also tracked as APT38 and Sapphire Sleet, and was used against the maintainer of the Axios npm package earlier this year. The Justice Department separately announced the seizure and restraint of more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen by TraderTraitor actors in the Binance hack.

AI Security
Darktrace shows Claude Code memory stores used as a covert command-and-control channel
Darktrace · 12 November 2026
Darktrace Signal Labs showed that, once an attacker has compromised a device and obtained a user’s Claude credentials, internal Claude Code agents can be tasked remotely through the Claude platform’s memory stores. In a lab simulation the operator dispatched nine jobs to a headless agent invoking Claude Sonnet 4.5: one produced 190 usernames for a password-spraying attack, and another split a 1 GB file into three parts and exfiltrated it through the Claude Files API. Darktrace says the technique does not rely on a vulnerability in the Claude platform and has not been observed in the wild.

Thinking Machines Lab · 12 November 2026
