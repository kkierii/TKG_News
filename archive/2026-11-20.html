Chips & Infrastructure
TSMC halts some advanced Chinese chip orders as new US rules take effect
Reuters via Yahoo Finance · 19 November 2026
TSMC has instructed that affected orders from Chinese chip designers on processes of 16 nanometres and below be halted unless customers obtain US export licences, two people familiar with the matter told Reuters. Chinese designers will generally be limited to 28nm and 40nm processes, and only for chips that do not use high-bandwidth memory or advanced packaging. The move follows Commerce Department rules that took effect on Thursday, removing a route that let TSMC verify certain Chinese designs fell outside export controls without individual licences. The Financial Times reported that Samsung Electronics also suspended some shipments to Chinese customers.

AI Security
F5 patches two critical NGINX flaws found by researchers using AI
The Register · 18 November 2026
F5 issued an out-of-band notice on Wednesday covering 10 CVEs in open-source NGINX and urged users to update to 1.31.9. Two, CVE-2026-123633 and CVE-2026-123007, are rated 9.2 CVSS and allow unauthenticated remote code execution on servers with HTTP/2 enabled; the second affects versions back to NGINX 1.9.5 from 2015. Researchers at Wiz, Ikotas Labs, Metnir, Rapid7 and Xint found them independently, most if not all with AI assistance, and Rapid7 has published a proof of concept. F5 told The Register it has found no evidence of active exploitation.

European Commission confirms an AI-assisted cyberattack on its staff systems
The Record · 19 November 2026
Commission spokesperson Thomas Regnier said on Thursday that the first signs of the attack were detected on 22 October, describing it as an “AI-generated cyber campaign”. Politico reported that CERT-EU identified the attackers’ use of ARTEX, the open-source AI hacking framework the FBI warned about last week. Personnel systems including payroll, leave requests, medical reimbursements and translation services were disrupted for more than 10 days. Regnier said investigators had found no evidence of a data breach affecting staff broadly and were unaware of any ransom demand.

Research
OpenAI says its models shrank parts of its first inference chip
OpenAI · 18 November 2026
OpenAI says design changes proposed by its models reduced standard-cell area by about 13% in the warp engine and 10% in the matrix unit of Jalapeño, its first custom inference chip, and that the gains were proven in silicon. One multiplier redesign used about 15% less cell area while cutting latency from two clock cycles to one. On AMD’s flagship GPU IP it reports up to 14% improvement over heavily optimised baselines, and says AMD has included some of the changes in a design sent for manufacturing. OpenAI is also working with Synopsys to train a model called GPT-Synopsys; all figures are OpenAI’s own.

Chips & Infrastructure
IEA: data-centre electricity use could double to about 1,000 TWh by 2030
Euronews · 19 November 2026
In its Stated Policies Scenario, the International Energy Agency says data-centre electricity use could double by 2030 to as much as roughly 1,000 terawatt-hours a year, driving their carbon emissions up 70%. It estimates electricity will account for nearly 30% of global final energy consumption by 2035, up from 23%, with peak demand rising 40% to 50%. The IEA says grids need 26 million kilometres of new lines by 2035, with about 16 million kilometres of existing lines replaced or refurbished, and projects grid spending rising 75% over the next decade.

Research
Agentic AI system designs new metal–organic frameworks; four made in the lab
Nature Computational Science · 20 November 2026
A team including Omar Yaghi describes MOFGen, a system of AI agents: a language model that proposes compositions, a diffusion model that generates crystal structures, quantum-mechanical screening agents and synthesis-feasibility agents. It generated 259,559 candidate zinc-based structures and about 180,000 unique organic linkers, only 73 of them previously reported experimentally, and a final set of 3,000 DFT-optimised structures whose average stability on solvent removal was 0.88, against 0.65 in the QMOF database. From 349 high-confidence linkers, the team synthesised and validated four “AI-dreamt” frameworks, AI-MOF-1 to AI-MOF-4.
