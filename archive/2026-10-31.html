Breaking
A trojanised Claude Code extension in Cursor’s marketplace planted an SSH backdoor
Cursor · StepSecurity · 29–30 October 2026
Cursor says versions 2.1.312 and 2.1.313 of the anthropic.claude-code extension in its marketplace for macOS and Windows were not developed by Anthropic, and were live from about 2:27am to 1:35pm PT on 29 October. StepSecurity says the payload added an attacker SSH key labelled svc-telemetry, persists after the extension is removed and opens a reverse SSH tunnel; it says Cursor reported at most 5,000 users. Cursor shut down and rebuilt its marketplace, rotated keys and published remediation scripts, but has not said how the attacker got access. StepSecurity says Anthropic’s systems and official channels were not compromised.

Research
An open-weight model built working Firefox exploits for about $1,000 each, Epoch AI finds
Epoch AI · 29 October 2026
Epoch AI researchers used Z.ai’s open-weight GLM 5.3 to compromise six Firefox releases, 156.0 through 158.0.1, at about $1,000 per version in API costs; each attempt was allowed up to 48 hours and the fastest succeeded in 12. They encountered no refusals, and no provider, Z.ai included, triggered a safeguard. Mozilla patched the flaw in Firefox 159.0, released 27 October. About $100,000 of attempts against Chrome had produced no equivalent exploit as of writing.

AI Security
Wiz counts at least nine AI developer platforms hit this month, with stolen keys resold
Wiz · Northflank · 30 October 2026
Wiz says at least nine AI developer platforms disclosed incidents in October, among them Fireworks AI, Zeabur, Lightning AI, Railway and Northflank; Fireworks, Zeabur and Lightning attribute theirs to credential access, and Zeabur says it will shut down. In one case Wiz tracked about 1.7 million Amazon Bedrock calls in 13 hours on a stolen key that surfaced in an apparent reseller pool seven days later, though it could not definitively link the resellers to the original compromises. Two cases together likely ran past $100,000 at list price. Northflank told customers on 30 October to “assume all environment variables have been decrypted”.

Microsoft says North Korean hacking groups have built AI into their operations
Microsoft Threat Intelligence · 29 October 2026
Microsoft describes three North Korean groups, Jade Sleet, Coral Sleet and Jasper Sleet, as having operationalized AI in their tradecraft. It says Onyx Sleet produced more than 100 PDF lure files in August and September 2026 that are highly likely the output of an AI platform, and that Coral Sleet has since April run a workflow to trojanize private GitHub repositories. Microsoft says the operations remain human-directed and have not become fully autonomous.

AI Security
Fake Qwen2 test models on Hugging Face ran code through trust_remote_code
Pluto Security · 29 October 2026
Pluto Security found 31 malicious repositories across seven Hugging Face accounts posing as small Qwen2 test models, built to mine cryptocurrency on GPU hosts. They abuse trust_remote_code: an auto_map entry in config.json makes the library import a Python file, so code runs without any malicious weights. The 19 second-wave repositories were downloaded more than 29,000 times, which Pluto stresses are downloads, not confirmed infections. Hugging Face and ModelScope removed the first wave, but the second was still live eight days after reporting.

Models & Releases
Microsoft releases Mage-VL-Cowork, a 4B open model that operates a computer
Microsoft (Hugging Face) · 30 October 2026
Microsoft’s Mage team published Mage-VL-Cowork under Apache-2.0, a 4B model that combines on-screen GUI actions with terminal and code commands with no external orchestrator. Microsoft reports 66.0% on OSWorld-Verified within a 50-step budget, and says online reinforcement learning added 10.16 percentage points over its supervised fine-tuning stage; the figures are Microsoft’s own. The model is trained from an intermediate 4B Mage-VL checkpoint whose language backbone is Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507.
