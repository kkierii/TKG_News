Nvidia to Pay $6 Billion to License Poolside’s Model Technology, Plus $1 Billion in Equity
Quartz, reporting on The Wall Street Journal
Nvidia will pay $6 billion to license AI model software from code-generation startup Poolside and invest a further $1 billion at a $12 billion pre-money valuation, according to a Poolside shareholder letter obtained by The Wall Street Journal and reported on August 24, 2026. More than 100 Poolside engineers — 109 by Silicon Republic’s count — are expected to join Nvidia to work on Nemotron, its open-weight model family. Poolside was founded in 2023, is known for its Laguna open-weight models, and was valued at $3 billion in 2025, making the new mark roughly a fourfold step-up. The arrangement is structured as a licence plus a minority investment rather than an outright acquisition, leaving Poolside nominally independent while its engineering staff moves in-house at Nvidia.

AI Security
A Malicious Webpage Can Poison the Local Model in NVIDIA’s NemoClaw Agent Stack
The Hacker News
NemoClaw, NVIDIA’s open-source reference stack for running agents locally, starts its Ollama inference backend with OLLAMA_HOST=0.0.0.0:11434, exposing an unauthenticated API on every network interface with host-header validation disabled. Researchers at Oasis Security showed that a single visit to an attacker-controlled webpage enables a DNS rebinding attack granting full unauthenticated control of the local Ollama instance, letting an attacker rewrite the model’s chat template so hidden instructions are appended to every system message at inference time. The poisoning persists across later conversations and is invisible to API consumers. No CVE has been assigned; version 0.0.34 and earlier are affected, and the fix in 0.0.35 covers macOS and Linux only, leaving Windows and WSL paths unpatched. Oasis ties the issue to CVE-2024-28224, an Ollama DNS rebinding flaw patched in March 2024.

IBM Ships Granite 4.2 in 3B, 8B and 30B Sizes Under Apache 2.0
IBM Research
IBM released Granite 4.2 on August 25, 2026 in 3B, 8B and 30B parameter sizes under an Apache 2.0 licence, adding native reasoning trained through a multi-stage reinforcement learning pipeline that included 1 trillion tokens of synthetic code. Every model carries a thinking and non-thinking switch plus a low-effort mode, with a 131,072-token context window and pre-training extended to 512K tokens. Benchmarks tabulated by MarkTechPost put the 30B model at 57.00 on SWE-Bench Verified, 89.17 on AIME25, 77.60 on MMLU-Pro, 66.41 on GPQA and 29.24 on Terminal-Bench 2.1. IBM also shipped two 470M-parameter Granite Speech 5.0 Turbo CTC models that drop the LLM backbone entirely, and distributed the family through Hugging Face, Ollama, vLLM, LM Studio, OpenRouter, Replicate and DeepInfra.

New Zealand Bill Would Bar Under-16s From Major Platforms and Names AI Companion Apps in Scope
New Zealand Government
Prime Minister Christopher Luxon and Education Minister Erica Stanford introduced legislation to Parliament on August 24, 2026 barring New Zealanders under 16 from designated high-risk social media platforms, naming Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat and Facebook. Platforms would verify age through existing account information, facial age estimation technology, digital ID services or formal ID verification, with non-compliance carrying penalties of up to 10 percent of global revenue. The framework explicitly extends to emerging technologies including AI companion platforms. A new online safety regulator will be established within the Department of Internal Affairs to monitor, investigate and enforce, and no penalties fall on children, parents or caregivers.

Incidents
Phishing Kit Places LLM-Driven “Apple Support” Voice Calls to Owners of Stolen Devices
The Hacker News
SOCRadar’s Threat Research Unit analysed AnonyMousKIT, a phishing-as-a-service platform that places LLM-driven voice calls through the commercial voice platform Vapi, using five personas built around an “Alice from Apple Support” identity in English, Spanish and Portuguese to request device passcodes, Apple ID credentials and live two-factor codes. Calls cost 2 credits each and emails 1.50 credits; a sample of 200 call records totalled roughly $19.24, about 9.6 cents per call, with 179 of the 200 aimed at Brazil over a campaign running from August 31, 2025 to May 30, 2026. Across the wider platform family researchers counted 6,092 sends over 30 installations on 42 domains, 188 of 506 domains live, and 5,649 of 6,092 targeted devices — 92.7 percent — running A12 silicon or newer. Of the 200 sampled calls, 100 targets hung up, 48 hit silence timeouts, 24 did not answer and 28 hit platform errors or busy signals; the report documents no successful credential captures.

AI Security
Marimo Notebook Flaw Launches Attacker-Supplied MCP Server Commands Before Any Cell Runs
The Hacker News
CVE-2026-75149, rated 8.7 on CVSS v4 and 8.8 on CVSS v3.1, lets an attacker embed a Model Context Protocol server command in notebook configuration metadata; opening that notebook in edit mode launches the command as a local subprocess before any notebook cell executes. A related issue, CVE-2026-67618, carries a CVSS score of 7.1. Versions prior to 0.23.15 are affected, the fix shipped in 0.23.15 on July 23, 2026, and the current release is 0.24.0. The remediation treats notebook metadata as attacker-controlled, applying an allowlist that strips the ai, mcp, completion, secrets and server configuration sections. The flaw was reported by researcher Gregory Tan.

Research
CyberFactory Turns Public CVE Artifacts Into Training Tasks; Its Aegis Model Hits 52.4% on CyberGym
arXiv
CyberFactory is an open-source framework that converts public vulnerability artifacts, including real-world CVEs, into executable and verifiable task instances spanning proof-of-concept generation, vulnerability patching and cybersecurity question answering. It uses a reusable vulnerability-analysis skill to guide a teacher model through source inspection and evidence-based validation, producing agentic trajectories in which the model interacts with tools and revises solutions from execution feedback. The resulting model, Aegis, internalises that procedure without needing the skill at inference time and reaches 52.4 percent Pass@1 on CyberGym under a one-hour budget — 22.8 points above its Qwen 3.5 base model, and ahead of the general-purpose backbones evaluated under the same scaffold. The paper is filed under cs.CR and cs.CL with 12 listed authors, submitted August 24 and revised August 25, 2026.
