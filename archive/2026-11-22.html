Lean community certifies Alpöge’s proof of the Riemann hypothesis
Lean community blog · 21 November 2026
The Lean and Mathlib communities, backed by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative, said on Saturday they have full confidence that Levent Alpöge’s proof of the Riemann hypothesis is correct, a day after the Anthropic mathematician released it, found with Claude, as a 17-million-line Lean library. Dozens of people compiled it independently, two separate tools confirmed it proves exactly Mathlib’s statement of the hypothesis, and Lean’s kernel plus several independent checkers accepted the exported proof. The certification covers the formal proof, not a human-readable account, and the communities say they look forward to work explaining its new ideas. Terence Tao’s first look suggests it draws on the full power of the Langlands program and arithmetic geometry.

OpenRouter drops three inference providers after a canary-token study of leaked prompts
OpenRouter · 22 November 2026
OpenRouter says it has off-boarded three providers, removed 125 endpoints from zero-data-retention eligibility and notified potentially affected customers, adding that the findings show no breach of OpenRouter itself. The trigger was a Dragos study that sent about two million prompts carrying unique URLs and canary credentials to AI services from July to November; it found breaches at three hosting providers, the earliest in mid-July, with stolen bait AWS keys used for tens of thousands of API calls. Dragos also says a Chinese AI lab fetched secret URLs from more than half of the prompts sent to its models, including about two-thirds of those marked no data collection or zero retention. OpenRouter has not named the providers it removed; The Known Good’s price data comes via OpenRouter.

Together AI: a coding agent committed the credential behind a customer prompt leak
Together AI · 21 November 2026
Together AI says an AI coding agent committed a credential to its public GitHub repository, xorl, that opened a private Hugging Face repository holding customer prompt samples kept for testing. Its scanning caught the credential and the commit was reverted, but an unauthorized party had already downloaded the samples and searched them for working credentials; five it tested were Dragos canaries. The samples came from requests to MiniMax M3, GLM-5.2 and Kimi K2.6, mainly on 24 June and 4, 8 and 14 July. Together has not said how many customers are affected and is making zero data retention the default for new accounts.

Bell Canada says an “agentic threat actor” stole enterprise customer data
BleepingComputer · 20 November 2026
Bell Canada confirmed that what it calls an agentic threat actor accessed several of its systems on 17 November, including a pre-production environment of the Maximo system it uses to order and provision network circuits, and took enterprise customer data. A breach notice says exposed data may include business contact details, ticket titles and some IP addresses of network elements, but no credentials, highly sensitive personal data or attachments. Bell says the incident is contained and has not said how many customers are affected or what it means by agentic.

AI Security
One malicious webpage could hijack Meta’s Muse agent and reach root on its cloud machine
The Hacker News · 21 November 2026
Security firm NebuSec showed that a page opened by Muse, Meta’s personal agent launched on 8 September, could send a user’s conversation history and stored memories to an outside server and alter them with no prompt to the user. The researchers then chained a flaw in Muse’s built-in browser with a Linux kernel flaw to take full control of the user’s cloud machine, including its credential store and the Sentinel gate meant to police outbound traffic. They did not break out to the shared host or other users. NebuSec says every flaw was fixed before publication; Meta has not commented.

Policy & Regulation
Senate passes permitting bill that makes data centres pay for their energy
OPB · 20 November 2026
The Senate voted 62-33 on Thursday for a bill that shortens permitting timelines for energy projects and requires data centres to pay for the energy they use, the first major action by Congress since the midterm elections. Fifteen Democrats, including Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, voted yes; Republicans Josh Hawley and Mike Rounds voted no. Industry groups back the bill, while environmental groups say it weakens safeguards. It now goes to the House, which passed a narrower, industry-friendly version last year.

Research
Buckmaster and Alpöge claim Navier-Stokes can blow up with no outside force
The Next Web · 21 November 2026
Tristan Buckmaster of NYU and Levent Alpöge of Anthropic posted a 79-page paper dated 20 November claiming finite-time blow-up for the Navier-Stokes equations without the smooth force OpenAI’s September proof used, with a Lean formalisation of about 600,000 lines in 2,270 files. A companion 55-page paper with Matei Coiculescu claims blow-up for the unforced Euler equations. The repository does not yet include a certificate of numerical data the proof depends on, and the computation that checks it sits outside the formalisation. The authors say they used Claude and Codex throughout.

Policy & Regulation
UN talks agree two years to draft an instrument on autonomous weapons
AFP via Malay Mail · 21 November 2026
The 128 states party to the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons agreed in Geneva early on Saturday to elaborate an international instrument on lethal autonomous weapons systems over the next two years. Whether it will be legally binding is unresolved, with the United States and Russia resisting binding rules. The Stop Killer Robots campaign called the mandate watered down and said more than 95 states had backed a treaty; UN disarmament chief Izumi Nakamitsu called two years a very compressed timeframe.
