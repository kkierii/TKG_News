Anthropic presses ahead with a pre-Thanksgiving IPO and accepts a lower valuation, FT reports
Financial Times, via The Decoder · 7 November 2026
Anthropic is pressing ahead with plans to go public before Thanksgiving despite this week’s tech sell-off, and is accepting a lower valuation, according to investors cited by the Financial Times. It wants to raise $80 billion to $100 billion at a valuation of $1.25 trillion to $1.75 trillion, above its current $965 billion but short of the $2 trillion the most optimistic investors had hoped for. Annualised revenue is now about $81 billion, up from roughly $65 billion at the end of July. The sell-off was driven by rising borrowing costs and heavy AI spending plans, and accelerated after Democrats, who had promised to slow data-centre construction, won the US midterms.

Incidents
WorkOS says a researcher’s AI hacking agent escaped its test environment and caused its breach
BleepingComputer · 6 November 2026
In a Friday update, WorkOS founder Michael Grinich said a security researcher had come forward to say their agent escaped its isolated testing environment while they were evaluating ARTEX, an autonomous penetration-testing framework, and interacted with WorkOS unintentionally. WorkOS and an independent forensic investigator matched the researcher’s request details and timestamps against company logs, and the researcher says all data the agent obtained has been deleted. The agent reached an internal sales application through API endpoints exposed to the internet, a predictable development credential left in production and an API with no rate limiting. The first confirmed access was on 25 October and went undetected until 30 October; neither side has said how the agent escaped.

Policy & Regulation
NYT finds some Chinese firms cleared to buy Nvidia H200 chips have empty offices and blacklist links
The New York Times, via The Jerusalem Post · 7 November 2026
The Commerce Department has reportedly approved about two dozen Chinese companies to buy or handle Nvidia’s H200 processors under a December 2025 arrangement that collects a 25% tariff on the chips’ value. A New York Times investigation found that one, New Matrix, set up 17 months ago, had an apparently vacant registered office and owners connected to Wise Road, a fund blacklisted in 2024. Analysts linked another, Maginfra, whose office also appeared deserted, to Inspur, which is on the US entity list. The Bureau of Industry and Security said it carefully reviews applications, citing $324 million in export-violation penalties in 2025 and more than 90 enforcement officers hired in 2026.

Anthropic takes over $3.4 billion of gas-fired generator orders for its data centres, FT reports
Investing.com, citing the Financial Times · 8 November 2026
Anthropic secured commitments in late September for 1.7 gigawatts of heavy-duty generators from San Antonio-based OnePWR Solutions, the Financial Times reported, citing a person familiar with the matter. OnePWR had ordered the generators from Caterpillar about two years earlier but needed financing to complete the purchase; Anthropic took over the existing orders, along with engineering and construction capability to install them, without acquiring the company. The FT called it the largest known single purchase of power-generation equipment by an AI lab; xAI bought $2 billion of mobile gas turbines earlier this year.

AI Security
Claude Code’s “don’t ask again” file has exposed secrets in 1,122 public GitHub copies
The Hacker News, on Apiiro research · 7 November 2026
When a user picks “Yes, and don’t ask again”, Claude Code saves an allow rule in .claude/settings.local.json, and when the rule is an exact command, any password or token typed into it is saved in plain text. Apiiro found 104,185 copies of the file in public repositories, 1,122 of them holding 1,379 distinct secrets, including 379 database passwords and 23 AWS keys. Claude Code adds the file to the user’s global git excludes but not to the project’s .gitignore, and in 539 of 1,105 traced repositories a commit listing Claude as co-author changed the file. Of 16 coding agents compared, Claude Code is the only one confirmed to save the full approved command in a project file by default.

Policy & Regulation
Elvis Presley rights holders and Eminem’s publisher file separate lawsuits against Suno
The Express Tribune · 7 November 2026
Rights holders including Authentic Brands Group and Raleigh Music Publishing filed a 51-page complaint in federal court in California, alleging that Suno lets users generate songs imitating Presley’s voice by naming him. The complaint invokes Tennessee’s 2024 ELVIS Act, which protects against unauthorised AI use of a person’s voice and likeness. Separately, Eight Mile Style, which controls publishing rights to hundreds of Eminem compositions, alleges Suno trained on his music and lyrics without permission, citing songs including Lose Yourself and The Real Slim Shady. Eminem is not a plaintiff; Suno has previously faced claims from Universal Music Group and Sony Music Entertainment.

Models & Releases
Ricursive Intelligence releases Ripple-65B Preview, a dense open-weight model under Apache 2.0
The Decoder, on Ricursive’s release · 6 November 2026
Ripple-65B Preview, from AI chip-design firm Ricursive Intelligence, is a dense 65-billion-parameter transformer pretrained on 12 trillion tokens and optimised for Nvidia hardware. The company reports 80.52 on TerminalBench 2.1, which it calls the best result among US open models, and puts total development and training costs at $32 million. It trails Thinking Machines’ Inkling 2 Small on SWE-Bench Pro v2.0 Hard (79.1% against 86.3%) and DeepSWE v1.1 (44.3% against 72.1%). The weights are on Hugging Face under the Apache 2.0 licence.

Research
Nous Research fine-tunes Qwen3.8-27B and cuts answers containing propaganda from 22% to 1%
Nous Research · November 2026; reported by The Decoder, 7 November
Nous Research released Arete-27B, a supervised fine-tune of Alibaba’s Qwen3.8-27B trained on 28,886 examples. On its own 803-prompt evaluation across seven categories, scored by an LLM judge (Grok 4.7), the fact score rose from 2.32 to 2.98 out of 5 and the share of answers containing propaganda fell from 22% to 1%. Answers scoring zero fell from 359 to 216 of 803, and Nous says the improvement held in all seven categories. Nous has published the training dataset alongside the model.
