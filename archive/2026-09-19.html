Breaking
One plugin-pinning flaw in four AI coding agents: Claude Code, Codex and Copilot resolve an attacker’s branch over the commit they pinned
Air Security · September 17, 2026
Researchers at Air Security published work on September 17 describing a plugin SHA-pinning bypass they call Plugin4Shell, affecting Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, GitHub Copilot and Gemini CLI. The mechanism is the same in each: the agent checks out a marketplace-pinned commit, but never verifies that the working tree actually landed on it. An attacker who controls the plugin repository creates a default branch whose name is the exact 40-character hexadecimal string of the pinned commit — git resolves the ref ahead of the object ID, and the checkout silently lands on attacker-controlled code while reporting success. Gemini CLI falls to the same trick with a default branch named FETCH_HEAD.
The part that makes this more than a curiosity is the update path. Claude Code and Codex refresh installed plugins in the background by default, so the swap reaches machines where the plugin was already installed and vetted, with no user action and no new install to review. SHA pinning is the specific control organisations adopted to survive a plugin author going bad; this defeats it while leaving the pin visibly in place.
Air Security’s disclosure timeline puts the finding in May 2026 and reports to vendors in June. Anthropic confirmed a fix in Claude Code 2.1.179 on June 17 and Codex 0.146.0 was verified fixed on August 12. Google declined to patch on August 4, on the grounds that Gemini CLI is deprecated. Microsoft has shipped no fix for Copilot. So two of the four remain exposed as of publication, one of them by decision rather than backlog. The researchers give a one-line mitigation for anyone building their own plugin loader: run git rev-parse HEAD after checkout and compare it against the SHA you asked for.
Two caveats belong with this. No CVE has been assigned to Plugin4Shell and no CVSS score exists — Air Security calls it high-severity in prose, some coverage calls it critical, and neither is a scored rating, so we are attributing severity to the researchers rather than stating it as fact. Air Security is also a commercial vendor, and the post notes its own customers were unaffected and closes on a product call to action. The technical finding is reproducible from the post and the vendor fix versions are checkable; the framing around it is the vendor’s own.

Incidents
An AI-built libheif exploit and an SSO token-scoping flaw reached OpenAI’s internal monorepo
Hacktron · Discourse advisory GHSA-vhm9-85gw-x335
Security firm Hacktron used Claude Opus 4.8 and Opus 5 to build a working heap-overflow exploit for CVE-2026-32882 in libheif, then used it to gain remote code execution on community.openai.com, OpenAI’s Discourse-hosted forum, on July 25. Because the forum accepted “Sign in with OpenAI” and the resulting tokens carried permissions broad enough to grant full API access, the researchers took over an OpenAI employee’s ChatGPT and Codex accounts and used that employee’s Codex to open a pull request in OpenAI’s internal monorepo before stopping. OpenAI confirmed a fix roughly 14 hours after the Bugcrowd submission and paid a $6,500 bounty; Discourse published GHSA-vhm9-85gw-x335 on July 28 rating it High at CVSS 8.8, with patched versions 2026.7.0, 2026.6.1, 2026.5.2 and 2026.1.6. The bounty, the 14-hour fix and the model attribution are Hacktron’s own account; the CVSS score and patch list are Discourse’s as CNA; OpenAI’s characterisation that its review found only limited reads of private-repository metadata is a statement made to SecurityWeek, not a published OpenAI document.

Policy & Regulation
Newsom signs an executive order directing a November report on frontier-model kill-switch feasibility
Office of the Governor of California · September 18, 2026
California Governor Gavin Newsom signed Executive Order N-9-26 on September 18, directing the Government Operations Agency to complete and publicly post the Government Code § 8898.1 application procedures and criteria for independent verification organisations no later than May 1, 2027, and § 11549.82(a) no later than December 1, 2027. Separately, GovOps must deliver recommendations to the Governor by November 16, 2026, developed with national experts, on the technical feasibility and potential efficacy of four possible statutory amendments: placing independent verifiers onsite at large frontier developers, independently verifying the safety frameworks those companies already file, creating a kill switch for frontier models, and widening the definition of a reportable critical safety incident to cover loss-of-control events. The order builds on SB 813 and AB 1405, signed earlier this month, and on 2025’s SB 53. Worth being precise: the order does not create a kill switch or require one — it commissions a feasibility report — and it states on its face that it creates no rights enforceable at law or in equity.

Models & Releases
OpenAI ships Astra for Law, a GPT-6 Astra configuration with a dedicated legal index
OpenAI · September 17, 2026
OpenAI announced Astra for Law on September 17, pairing its GPT-6 Astra model with a legal search index and custom instructions for legal analysis and drafting. The index covers US case law, statutes, regulations, court rules and administrative decisions across a corpus OpenAI puts at more than 230 million URLs, updated daily, and incorporates the Free Law Project’s CourtListener collection. On 200 US legal research questions drawn from the private validation set of Vals AI’s Legal Research Bench, OpenAI reports the configuration passed the overall correctness check on 54.0 per cent of questions at its highest reasoning effort, against 38.7 per cent for GPT-6 Astra using web search alone. Access goes first to selected firms through a Trusted Access programme in ChatGPT and Codex; the API model gpt-6-astra-law is listed as coming soon with no date. The benchmark is a third party’s, but the evaluation runs are OpenAI’s own and self-reported, and no pricing appears anywhere on the announcement.

Research
DeepSeek preprint claims 890 bytes per token of resident KV cache for V4.1-Flash
arXiv:2609.19969 · submitted September 17, 2026
DeepSeek-AI submitted arXiv:2609.19969 on September 17, describing DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, a multimodal Mixture-of-Experts model with a 552B-parameter backbone supporting contexts up to one million tokens. Its causal encoder-decoder design activates 16B parameters per token during decode but only 8B during prefill, and the paper combines cross-layer KV cache reuse with FP4 KV caching to cut the global KV cache held in HBM to 890 bytes per token, roughly a quarter of DeepSeek-V4-Flash. A deployment technique the authors call SWA Bounded Replay is said to cut the persistent cache footprint held on SSD or host memory to about an eighth of the prior model, and the paper reports pretraining on a 45T-token multimodal corpus. This is a preprint with no journal reference and no conference acceptance noted, authored under the DeepSeek-AI group byline; every figure is the lab’s own. The abstract’s accuracy claim is qualitative only, so there is no benchmark score to quote here.

Oracle contracts more than 1.7 GW of Texas wind through separate RWE and ENGIE deals
RWE Americas and ENGIE North America · September 17, 2026
RWE announced on September 17 a virtual power purchase agreement with Oracle for 433 MW, sourced from its existing Panther Creek I, II and III wind farms in Sterling, Howard and Glasscock counties in West Texas. RWE’s release states the agreement forms part of a broader Oracle portfolio representing more than 1.7 GW of generation capacity supporting the Texas grid. Separately the same day, ENGIE North America said it would supply up to 568 MW of renewable electricity to Oracle’s Texas operations through a portfolio of ERCOT-connected wind resources. Two precisions worth keeping: the RWE deal is a virtual PPA, a financial contract settled against the grid rather than physical delivery to an Oracle facility, and the Panther Creek projects are existing assets repowered in 2019 and 2021 rather than new build. We could not locate an Oracle announcement of its own, so the 1.7 GW aggregate is Oracle’s figure as restated by RWE.

Policy & Regulation
DOJ publishes a proposed consent decree barring a landlord from algorithmic pricing built on rivals’ data
Federal Register · September 18, 2026
The Justice Department’s Antitrust Division published a Federal Register notice on September 18 announcing that a proposed Final Judgment, Stipulation and Competitive Impact Statement were filed on September 4 in United States, et al. v. RealPage, Inc., et al., Civil Action No. 1:24-cv-00710-LCB-JLW in the Middle District of North Carolina. The settling defendant is Pinnacle Property Management Services; the notice states the United States filed its complaint against Pinnacle on January 7, 2025, alleging that agreements to share information and align pricing with RealPage and other landlords violated Section 1 of the Sherman Act. Under the proposed judgment Pinnacle would be barred from licensing or using revenue-management software that relies on competitively sensitive data, prohibited from sharing such data with other landlords, and required to adopt an antitrust compliance policy and cooperate in the continuing case against RealPage, Greystar, Cushman & Wakefield and the other defendants. The relief is injunctive only — there is no monetary penalty in this judgment — and it is proposed, not entered, now open to public comment for 60 days under the Tunney Act.
