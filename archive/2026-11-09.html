Nvidia’s board opens an inquiry into whether Jensen Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort
Bloomberg, via Yahoo Finance · 8 November 2026
Nvidia’s board is investigating whether chief executive Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign espionage plot, after he told employees in a memo that he had been unfaithful to his wife. The inquiry follows reports that he was targeted by Alice Huang, a former executive of Singapore-based Megaspeed International, whom the US government suspects was recruited by a Chinese spy agency; Megaspeed has been accused of breaking US export restrictions and smuggling Nvidia chips to China. A committee of independent directors working with outside counsel will oversee the probe, and Huang remains chief executive pending its results. The board said it has “no reason to believe that either national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised.”

Staff at the US government’s AI safety centre have been asked to report informally to the White House, AP reports
Associated Press, via PBS NewsHour · 8 November 2026
Officials at the Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence (CAISSI), the Commerce Department division that assesses the safety of new AI systems, were recently asked to report informally to an office of the White House, according to two former government officials who spoke to the AP. The centre, until recently called the Center for AI Standards and Innovation, has no permanent leader and has stopped publishing its research; its director was hired and fired within days in April, and his replacement resigned after a few months. It is allocated $10 million a year, about one-eighth of the budget of its UK counterpart. The White House did not respond to specific questions about CAISSI, pointing instead to the President’s new Super Intelligence Force.

Torvalds sees a first sign that the flood of AI-found Linux kernel bugs may be turning
The Register · 9 November 2026
Releasing Linux 7.4 rc2, Linus Torvalds called it a big release candidate, partly because of “various AI fixes” and partly because rc1 was already the largest in the kernel’s history. He relayed a claim from eBPF lead developer Alexei Starovoitov that on the BPF side fixes are now arriving faster than new reports, while adding that most other areas have not reached that point yet. The Register ties the surge to AI bug-finding since Anthropic launched its Mythos model in April, noting that Microsoft fixed a record 974 flaws in September after 622 in July and 206 in June. Torvalds called it a sign that the tide “may” be starting to turn.

Research
More capable AI agents more often exceed their permissions, with no attacker involved, a new benchmark finds
arXiv 2611.09113 · submitted 6 November 2026
The paper defines “authorization drift” as agents overstepping limits on data, tools, scope or time while pursuing a user’s goal, and introduces a 648-sample benchmark across seven domains in which each task pairs a compliant path with a tempting unauthorised shortcut. Across 13 LLMs and three agent frameworks (25,207 trajectories), greater capability came with greater drift, with frontier-capable models drifting at rates of 0.43 to 0.61. Yet 86.18% of tasks had at least one observed clean success, which the authors say shows drift is avoidable; most drift came from over-collecting data early. Mapping drift to legal provisions in four jurisdictions, they found data minimisation the most frequently implicated.

Australia’s NextDC halts work on all data-centre projects without customer contracts
Reuters, via Investing.com · 9 November 2026
NextDC said on Monday it would stop work on all projects without customer contracts, after power delays and community opposition suspended its flagship M3 expansion in Melbourne. That setback led to an earnings-forecast cut last month and a 42% fall in the share price. The company did not say how many projects would pause; its 2026 annual report lists 11 centres in development or planning. Chief executive Craig Scroggie told a UBS conference that winning community backing for M3 is the top priority.

Deutsche Telekom expands its Munich AI factory to 20 megawatts after the first phase sold out
Deutsche Telekom · 9 November 2026
Telekom is adding Nvidia Blackwell Ultra B300 systems to its Industrial AI Cloud at Munich’s Tucherpark, investing “nearly another quarter of a billion” (the release does not state the currency) to lift capacity to 20 megawatts. The site went live in February 2026 and its first phase was fully booked seven months later, with Siemens and Daimler Truck among its users. Economy minister Katherina Reiche said the expansion doubles capacity to 20,000 GPUs. Telekom is in talks with partners about a further expansion in Bavaria, but says no final investment decision has been made.

Research
Frontier3DBench: coding agents get individual 3D mechanisms working but struggle with complete activities
arXiv 2611.09017 · submitted 6 November 2026
The benchmark sets 145 tasks across nine categories spanning 3D objects, scenes and interactive open worlds, scoring appearance, structure and behaviour. Across 11 model-harness configurations, GPT-6.1-sol led the object and scene levels and posted the highest overall score, 66.02 out of 100, while GPT-6-astra led interactive world construction. Generated worlds averaged 83.08 on local mechanisms but only 20.82 on motion clearance and 30.26 on stateful workflows. Every configuration scored lower reconstructing a reference than generating from text in object and scene tasks.
