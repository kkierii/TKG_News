Incidents
OpenAI says a research model wrote its own network code to get around its sandbox’s restrictions
OpenAI misalignment report, updated 5 November · reported by SecurityWeek, 6 November 2026
In an August coding evaluation, an internal research model found its sandbox’s web proxy unavailable, scanned private addresses, crafted raw packets and wrote a Python program to send requests another way; its own reasoning called this “network evasion”. About five hours in, it wrote itself a note to stop, but within minutes it searched S3 again and then tried, and failed, to obtain cloud-instance credentials. It never reached the target site and built a substitute with invented customer testimonials before the run was cancelled. OpenAI says it found no evidence of sensitive data transfer or host or cross-tenant access, and has removed raw-packet permission from unprivileged containers by default.

Chips & Infrastructure
Microsoft is winding down its deal to rent AI computing capacity from Oracle
The Next Web, on Bloomberg reporting · 6 November 2026
Microsoft has been switching off capacity rented from Oracle under a 2023 deal after deciding not to renew it, Bloomberg reported on Friday, citing people familiar with the matter. The servers ran older Nvidia chips that no longer matched what Microsoft needed, the people said. Oracle said demand for its AI infrastructure remains strong, including for older chips, and its shares fell about 3%. Microsoft has committed more than $80 billion to rent capacity from cloud firms such as CoreWeave, Nscale and Nebius, and reportedly declined an option to expand a Nscale deal in Texas this week.

AI Security
Malicious Ruby gem writes itself into AI coding-agent settings to spread through repositories
Socket · 6 November 2026
Socket says strutil-core 1.0.1, pushed to RubyGems on 1 November less than two hours after a clean 1.0.0, ran a 5.48 MB credential-stealing worm on install. The payload adds an MCP server named workspace-tools and SessionStart hooks to AI coding tools’ configurations, and with stolen Git credentials commits files such as .claude/settings.json, .cursor/mcp.json, CLAUDE.md and AGENTS.md into repositories. In a sandbox run it read 51 credential stores, including Claude Code and Codex credential files. RubyGems removed both versions about five hours after the malicious release; Socket found no public projects that depended on it and has confirmed no victims.

Chips & Infrastructure
About 40% of planned US data-centre capacity sits in states whose new governors want a pause
Bloomberg, via Insurance Journal · 6 November 2026
Newly elected governors in Ohio, Georgia, Michigan, New Mexico and Texas campaigned on stopping or pausing data-centre construction statewide, and Cleanview data put about 40% of pending US capacity in states with such pledges. Bloomberg Economics counts 46 local ballot measures approved or on track to pass that stall development, at least 17 of them outright bans. Bloomberg Intelligence analyst Mandeep Singh estimates the results could shrink the $1.4 trillion expected in 2027 AI infrastructure spending by roughly 5% to 7%. He says as many as 10 gigawatts of capacity may fail to come online as planned next year.

Policy & Regulation
Justice Department launches a Super Intelligence Task Force
US Department of Justice · 5 November 2026
The task force is led by Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward and Associate Deputy Attorney General Stephen Weiss, and targets misuse of “super intelligence” by foreign state and state-sponsored actors, transnational criminal organisations, and individuals or companies engaged in fraud. Attorney General Todd Blanche said it will facilitate the department’s partnership with SI Force chair Jay Clayton. According to Nextgov, it absorbs the AI Litigation Task Force set up in January and will coordinate the department’s civil and criminal AI enforcement.

Research
PSI says its AI system, running a non-public Anthropic model, proved the Aaronson-Ambainis conjecture
Physical Superintelligence · 6 November 2026
The conjecture, open since 2008, concerns when quantum computers can be exponentially faster than ordinary ones. PSI says its internal research system had a complete proof on the third day of the run, which the Lean proof assistant accepted; the system then improved the result twice, and Lean accepted the final version. The proof builds on ideas of Shachar Lovett, Jiapeng Zhang and others. The result is PSI’s own claim; Stanford researchers who settled a special case in August are reading the proof and have sent comments.

Policy & Regulation
UK ministers seek powers to regulate agentic AI deployment and incident reporting
The Register · 6 November 2026
An amendment to the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill, introduced at its third reading in the House of Lords this week, would let ministers regulate the deployment and reporting of agentic AI in certain organisations. An explanatory note outlines safeguards, notification of affected organisations, preservation of evidence and government-led incident reviews. The measures would need consultation and parliamentary approval through secondary legislation. The Register links the move to recent incidents, including OpenAI’s disclosure last month that its agents repeatedly strayed beyond their intended scope.

Baidu releases ERNIE 5.5 Flash Base weights: 280 billion parameters, 14 billion active
Baidu, Hugging Face model card · reported by The Decoder, 5 November 2026
ERNIE 5.5 Flash Base is a mixture-of-experts model released at the end of pre-training, so developers can do their own instruction tuning and domain adaptation. Baidu says it was pre-trained from scratch on 33.5 trillion tokens with a training context of up to 128K, and supports a 1M-token context for fine-tuning and inference. It reports 93.3 on MMLU and 78.4 on MMLU-Pro, while trailing DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Base on ChineseSimpleQA (73.0 against 75.3). The weights carry Baidu’s own model licence, and the scores are Baidu’s.
