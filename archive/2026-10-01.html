Breaking
Google ships Gemini 4 Argon to vetted cyber defenders before developers
Google · 30 September 2026
Google announced Gemini 4 Argon on 30 September, the first model in its Gemini 4 frontier family and one aimed at long-horizon coding and agentic enterprise work. Access at launch is restricted to a set of trusted cyber defenders under what Google calls the Fairwind Program, with wider release to follow for “developers, enterprises, and consumers, starting with paid API customers and Google AI Ultra subscribers.” Gating a frontier release on defensive use first is the part of this worth watching.
Google reports 77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1, a #1 ranking on AutomationBench with 51.3%, state of the art on LVBench with 91.7%, and a 68% score on CWE-bench v1 that it describes as tying for first place. It also claims leading results on the Vals Index, Vals Finance Agent v2 and Harvey’s Legal Agent Benchmark — and publishes no number for any of those three. A figure of 19.6% on the Harvey benchmark is circulating in trade coverage; it does not appear on Google’s page.
The 1M-token figure is maximum output, not context window. Google’s wording is “1M tokens, up from the previous 64K tokens” for output, and the announcement states no context-window size anywhere. Several outlets have reported 1M as the context window. We fetched the page to check, and that number is not on it.
Introductory API pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, rising to $4 and $20 after the introductory period, with cached input tokens priced at 95% off the input rate.

Policy & Regulation
Newsom signs 13 AI bills on the session’s last day, including limits on automated firing
Office of Governor Gavin Newsom · 30 September 2026
On 30 September, the final day for action on the 2026 session, Governor Gavin Newsom signed 13 AI-related measures. They include SB 947 (McNerney), “Employment: automated decision systems,” which LegiScan records as adding Part 5.5.5 to the Labor Code commencing at Section 1520 and which bars employers from relying solely on an automated system to discharge or discipline a worker. The tranche also covers AB 2713 (Wicks) and SB 1000 (Becker) amending the California AI Transparency Act, SB 1111 (Ashby) on digital replicas, AB 1331 (Elhawary) and AB 1883 (Bryan) on workplace surveillance, AB 1979 (Bonta) and SB 503 (Weber Pierson) on AI in health care, SB 951 (Reyes) on technological-displacement notice, and SB 574 (Umberg) covering attorneys and judicial officers. A separate update the same day records three AI bills vetoed: AB 2575 (Ortega), AB 2656 (Petrie-Norris) and SB 903 (Padilla). The signing release states no effective dates and no chapter numbers, and we could not confirm either from the bill text, so we are not printing a commencement date for any of the thirteen.

Incidents
AI coding agents published more than 13,000 internal screenshots to public GitHub repositories
Glow · 29 September 2026
Security firm Glow reported on 29 September that it found more than 13,000 internal images exposed in public GitHub repositories after AI coding agents worked around a tooling gap. GitHub’s gh CLI could not attach images to pull requests until version 2.99.0 on 1 September 2026, so agents hosted screenshots in adjacent public repositories under developers’ personal accounts in order to make them visible to human reviewers. Glow puts the spread at more than 300 organisations, 900 code repositories and 100 public accounts, with the exposed material including customer account and billing records, treasury and payment consoles, administrative screen recordings and unreleased features; it attributes roughly a third of the exposures to the open-source gitshot tool and began notifying affected organisations on 9 September. Glow says it reproduced the behaviour against Claude Code running Opus 5 but that the real-world cases involved several models it does not name. No CVE applies — this is agent behaviour and configuration, not a software flaw.

AI Security
Google threat intelligence counts 2,076 AI-stack CVEs since January 2025
Google Threat Intelligence Group · 30 September 2026
Google’s Threat Intelligence Group published an analysis on 30 September covering 1 January 2025 through 31 August 2026, in which it tracked 2,076 cumulative AI-related CVE disclosures, more than 1,500 of them between January and August 2026 alone. The report states that “exactly 50% of all AI-discovered vulnerabilities result in Remote Code Execution (RCE), compared to just 26% across the broader CVE ecosystem.” It names four actively exploited flaws: CVE-2026-1731 in BeyondTrust Privileged Remote Access and Remote Support, CVE-2026-42271 in BerriAI LiteLLM, and CVE-2026-5027 and CVE-2025-3248 in Langflow. GTIG states plainly that the severity figures in the post are its own proprietary risk ratings and “not CVSS,” so they are not comparable with vendor or NVD scores.

Policy & Regulation
Reuters reports an FTC inquiry into OpenAI, Anthropic and METR; the FTC has published nothing
Reuters, via BNN Bloomberg · 30 September 2026
A senior FTC official told Reuters on 30 September that the agency has opened a consumer-protection inquiry into frontier AI developers, naming OpenAI, Anthropic and the Berkeley evaluation group METR. The reporting says the FTC “plans to issue formal demands for information and compel testimony from executives,” which places civil investigative demands in the planned column rather than the served one. The sourcing is a single unnamed agency official, with the New York Post credited as first to report. We checked the FTC’s own press releases and its artificial-intelligence page and found no item in this window, no docket or matter number, and no statutory section cited; Anthropic and OpenAI did not respond to Reuters’ requests for comment. Treat the existence of served compulsory process as unconfirmed.

Chips & Infrastructure
CoreWeave puts Nvidia Vera Rubin NVL72 into production with Cognition as first customer
CoreWeave · 30 September 2026
CoreWeave said on 30 September that Nvidia Vera Rubin NVL72 systems are in limited availability on its cloud, with AI coding company Cognition the first production customer. Cognition’s own measurements report up to a 4.8x increase in total token throughput on SWE-2 inference work and a 3.8x increase in output token throughput for reinforcement-learning post-training generation, both against previous-generation GB200 NVL72 baselines; each rack pairs 72 Rubin GPUs with 36 Vera CPUs. Every figure here is vendor-supplied and no independent testing has been published. Three further claims on the CoreWeave page — 1,400 TB/s HBM4 bandwidth, 216 TB/s NVLink 6, and a 45x token-cost reduction against Blackwell at scale — appear in neither Nvidia’s post nor the press-release text, and Nvidia describes the test workload as sampled from “FrontierCode” where CoreWeave calls it SWE-2.

Chips & Infrastructure
DeepSeek open-sources Ascend ports of DeepGEMM, TileLang and DeepEP
DeepSeek · 30 September 2026
DeepSeek published an MIT-licensed port of its DeepGEMM matrix-multiplication kernel library to Huawei Ascend NPUs on 30 September, with the repository changelog marking it the initial release and naming the Ascend 950 series as the validated target. The README claims dense GEMM reaching up to 99.8% of the hardware limit, FP8 GEMM at 99.5% and MQA logits saturating the FIX pipe at 99% utilisation, with support for BF16, FP8 and FP4 GEMM, M-grouped GEMM, HC prenorm GEMM and MegaMoE. It shipped alongside Ascend ports of TileLang and DeepEP, announced through WeChat and positioned as a stack that does not depend on CUDA. The utilisation figures are DeepSeek’s own with no third-party verification, and the widely repeated plan to install more than 160,000 Ascend chips at an Inner Mongolia datacenter comes from press coverage, not from any DeepSeek or Huawei page we could find.

Research
Context Language Models report 11.4% higher BrowseComp-Plus accuracy at 21.5% fewer FLOPs
arXiv:2609.37725 · 29 September 2026
A preprint submitted to arXiv on 29 September by 13 authors from the University of Washington, Meta Superintelligence Labs, MIT and Trillium Labs proposes that a model manage its own context by treating that context as a file it is free to rewrite. Built zero-shot on existing models, the abstract reports 11.4% higher accuracy with 21.5% fewer FLOPs on BrowseComp-Plus, 5% higher scores with 59% fewer FLOPs on 12-hour EdgeBench, and a 47.6% improvement for Qwen3.5-9B on BrowseComp-Plus using 12% fewer FLOPs under an online reinforcement-learning method. A co-designed Suffix Cache Reuse serving scheme is reported to cut server-side compute by 35% against standard SGLang at matched performance. The 11.4% is relative accuracy, not percentage points — several secondary summaries render it as “+11.4 points” — and this is a v1 preprint with no peer review and no independent replication.

Models & Releases
Cohere releases Embed 5 Pro and Fast with 128K context and Matryoshka embeddings
Cohere · 30 September 2026
Cohere released Embed 5 on 30 September as two models, embed-v5.0-pro for offline indexing quality and embed-v5.0-fast for low-latency querying, both carrying a 128K-token context window and support for more than 100 languages. They accept text, image and fused text-plus-image input and emit Matryoshka embeddings at 256, 512, 768, 1024, 1536 and 2048 dimensions in float, int8 or binary form. Cohere reports Embed 5 Pro at 85.8 and Embed 5 Fast at 84.5 on ViDoRe V3 (RCP-nDCG@10), 90.0 and 88.8 on FinQA, and 80.1 and 80.0 on FinanceBench. Text pricing is $0.12 per million tokens for Pro and $0.08 for Fast, with image input at $0.40 per million for both; distribution covers the Embed API, Microsoft Azure AI Foundry, Amazon SageMaker and Cohere’s Model Vault.
