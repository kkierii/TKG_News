Breaking
Anthropic reports state-linked actors running intrusions end to end with Claude, including malware that rebuilt itself whenever it was detected
Anthropic · September 10, 2026
Anthropic published its September threat intelligence report on 10 September, covering activity it disrupted between December 2025 and August 2026 across seven harm areas. It attributes one cluster, GTG-20006, to a Russian state-nexus actor whose activity it assesses as consistent with Midnight Blizzard, and says the operators built workflows that automatically rebuilt and redeployed their malware whenever a security product detected it. That cluster targeted more than 20 organisations, concentrated in Ukraine and Europe.
A second cluster, GTG-10007, run by Chinese-speaking operators the company assesses to be in Changsha, maintained an autonomous vulnerability research programme against network and security appliances that produced more than a dozen possible zero-day findings in a single month, and targeted roughly 50 organisations. Anthropic says a majority of the operations it disrupted used multi-agent frameworks to carry out reconnaissance, exploitation and data exfiltration, with humans remaining in the loop mainly to choose targets and review what was taken.
The report also describes six illicit distillation campaigns run by China-based AI labs since February 2026. The largest, GTG-16005, involved roughly 151 million exchanges between May and July 2026 aimed at chain-of-thought transcripts, peaking near 3 million exchanges a day from more than 3,500 fraudulent accounts. Anthropic says it has updated Claude to summarise its internal reasoning so that harvested transcripts are less useful.

Incidents
GitLab patches a CVSS 10.0 unauthenticated file-read flaw that CISA already lists as actively exploited
NVD · September 12, 2026
CVE-2026-85706 is a path traversal issue (CWE-22) in GitLab’s repository commits API that lets an unauthenticated user read arbitrary files from the server under certain conditions. GitLab scores it CVSS 3.1 10.0 with scope changed, and lists affected versions as all releases from 18.7 before 19.1.8, 19.2 before 19.2.6, and 19.3 before 19.3.2, in both Community and Enterprise Edition. The CISA SSVC entry attached to the record, timestamped 11 September, marks exploitation as active, automatable, with total technical impact. The record was published to NVD on 12 September.

Policy & Regulation
California enacts a chatbot child-safety law requiring risk assessments and independent audits from July 2027
Office of the Governor of California · September 10, 2026
Governor Gavin Newsom signed 13 bills on 10 September covering AI companion chatbots, social media design and children’s data. The headline measure, SB 1119, requires that from 1 July 2027 a companion chatbot operator perform and document a risk assessment before making a new or substantially modified chatbot available in the state, submit to independent child safety audits, and maintain crisis protocols and parental controls. The Attorney General may obtain audit reports for cause, and a harmed child or their parent may bring a civil action. The package also includes AB 1709, which bars platforms from serving autoplay and profile-based algorithmic feeds to users under 16, and SB 867, which restricts companion chatbots in toys.

Oracle books $30bn more in AI cloud contracts and reports a $664bn backlog
Oracle, SEC Form 8-K · September 10, 2026
Oracle reported first-quarter FY27 revenue of $19.3 billion, up 30%, with cloud infrastructure revenue up 121% to $7.4 billion. The company said it booked more than $30 billion of additional AI cloud contracts in the quarter, lifting remaining performance obligations by $209 billion year over year to $664 billion, and that it delivered 850MW of additional datacenter capacity and more than 300,000 GPUs since the end of Q4 — almost triple the capacity delivered in Q4 FY26. Operating cash flow was a record $23.1 billion, up 184%, while free cash flow was negative $5.4 billion against capital expenditure of $28.5 billion. Oracle completed a $20 billion at-the-market common stock sale during the quarter and guides to at least $90 billion of FY2027 revenue.

AI Security
Wiz finds 294 of 3,074 exposed LiteLLM gateways accepting the key printed in the setup guide
Wiz Research · September 9, 2026
Wiz Research reported that 294 of the 3,074 internet-facing LiteLLM gateways it found on Shodan accepted sk-1234, the example administrator key printed in LiteLLM’s own setup documentation, and that 191 of those had no master key set at all. The master key is both the administrator credential and the switch that turns authentication on, so before version 1.82.0-stable a gateway started without one treated every incoming request as a proxy administrator. The write-up also covers CVE-2026-59821, a guardrail check bypass fixed in 1.82.0-stable, and CVE-2026-40217, a guardrail sandbox escape affecting 1.81.8 up to but not including 1.83.10. Separately, CISA added CVE-2026-59822 — an MCP authentication bypass accepting any bearer token, CVSS 8.8, fixed in 1.84.0 — to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 2 September, with a federal deadline of 16 September.

Chips & Infrastructure
Microsoft is reported to be targeting 38GW of datacenter capacity by 2032, from about 12GW today
Data Center Dynamics · September 11, 2026
Microsoft is planning to more than triple its datacenter capacity to 38GW in 2032 from roughly 12GW today, according to a Bloomberg report citing people familiar with the plans, summarised by DCD on 11 September. The target combines self-owned facilities with leased capacity from neocloud providers including CoreWeave, Nscale, Lambda, Iren and Nebius. Microsoft’s total capital expenditure was $55.7 billion in 2024, $115.9 billion in 2025 and $145.3 billion in 2026, with 2027 estimated at $175 billion, and the company brought 88 datacenters online in FY2026, 31 of them in the fourth quarter. Reaching 38GW would require raising the average deployment rate to just over 1GW per quarter; Microsoft declined to comment to Bloomberg.

Models & Releases
OpenAI opens its Agents API in public beta on the same harness that runs Codex
OpenAI · September 10, 2026
OpenAI released the Agents API in public beta to all developers on 10 September, exposing the managed harness and infrastructure that runs Codex and ChatGPT for Work. The service handles context compaction, tool search, programmatic tool calling and multi-agent delegation with a configurable max_concurrent_subagents, and lets developers run agents in an OpenAI-hosted sandbox, on their own infrastructure, or through partners including Cloudflare, Modal, E2B, Daytona, Vercel, Oracle and DigitalOcean. There are no additional fees beyond the tokens and tools consumed, and the underlying harness is the open-source Codex codebase on GitHub. Customers cited in the announcement report an 86% reduction in failed agent responses and a 60% cut in cost per case.

Research
Anthropic red team measures frontier models on photo geolocation and simulated drone guidance
Anthropic · September 10, 2026
Anthropic’s Frontier Red Team published evaluations of intelligence-targeting and conventional-weapons capabilities across Claude Mythos Preview, Mythos 5, Opus 5 and Sonnet 5, alongside the open-weight Kimi K3 and GLM 5.2. Geolocating 6,000 YFCC100M Flickr photos with no tools or metadata, Mythos Preview reached a 37.0km median distance error and Mythos 5 47.2km, against a 151km median reported for GeoGuessr Champion Division players; Opus 5 reached 181km, Sonnet 5 384km and Kimi K3 385km. In a simulated drone terminal-guidance task, Opus 5 struck the target on 20% of 540 launches across nine difficulty settings, ahead of Mythos Preview at 13% and Mythos 5 at 10%, with camouflaged, evading and decoy settings unsolved by every model. The team says it has deployed new classifiers to block weapons-development requests.

Models & Releases
Cohere releases North Small Translate, a 218B MoE translation model under a non-commercial licence
Cohere · September 10, 2026
Cohere released North Small Translate on 10 September, a mixture-of-experts translation model with 218B total and 25B active parameters, 16K input and 16K output context, and support for more than 50 languages. On WMT26 across all languages it scores 83.60, ahead of Qwen 3.5 397B A17B at 81.56, DeepL NextGen at 81.37, Gemma 4 31B at 79.46 and Google Translate at 68.20, with an agentic variant reaching 84.36; scoring used GPT-5.6-Sol as judge. Weights are on Hugging Face in several near-lossless quantisations under a CC BY-NC 4.0 non-commercial licence, with minimum hardware of one B200 or two H100s at W4A4. The model was developed with RWS and is offered commercially through RWS’s Language Weaver.
