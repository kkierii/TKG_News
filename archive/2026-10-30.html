CrowdStrike says a China-nexus group ran AI agents against more than 200 organisations in 11 days
CrowdStrike · 29 October 2026
CrowdStrike says a China-nexus group, VAPOR PANDA, used ARTEX, an agentic framework running primarily on DeepSeek, against more than 200 organisations in at least 14 countries in 11 days from 17 October. Targets included foreign ministries, an interior ministry, and defence, semiconductor, pharmaceutical, nuclear and telecom organisations; CrowdStrike confirmed post-exploitation at nine and names two where the BLOODALCHEMY backdoor was deployed. A human operator stayed in the loop, issuing instructions in Chinese, while planning agents consumed on the order of 1.4 billion input tokens in a single intrusion. CrowdStrike says the campaign is separate from ARTEX activity reported under the Storm-0940 designation, and assesses that China-nexus adversaries will highly likely keep adopting such frameworks over the next 12 months.

Models & Releases
Mistral releases the weights of Mistral Large 4, a 1-trillion-parameter model
Mistral AI · TechCrunch · 29 October 2026
Mistral has published open weights for Mistral Large 4, a 1-trillion-parameter hybrid instruct-and-reasoning mixture-of-experts model with multimodal input, under a modified MIT licence that requires Model-as-a-Service providers to obtain a separate commercial licence. API pricing is $0.70 per million input tokens and $2.10 output. Mistral reports 41% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, up 13 percentage points from the public preview, and 48% on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index; the benchmark figures are Mistral’s own. The weights are on Hugging Face, and Microsoft Foundry is the first cloud provider to offer the model.

UK Budget puts £700 million toward AI risk, including £120 million more for the AI Security Institute
HM Treasury (GOV.UK) · 28 October 2026
Chancellor John Healey’s Budget says the government is investing £700 million to tackle the risks of AI, including an additional £120 million for the AI Security Institute and £40 million for a national capability to investigate and respond to agentic security incidents. It also gives the Sovereign AI Unit an additional £140 million and puts £30 million into piloting local AI partnerships, starting with three Yorkshire combined authorities. A new Data Centre Covenant sets tests on community investment, energy costs, British industry and jobs, and national security that strategic data centre projects must meet to get enhanced government support.

AI Security
Built-in browsers in five AI coding tools could reach the machine behind them; Codex and VS Code remain unpatched
Forever Security · The Register · 29 October 2026
Forever Security researcher Gal Weizman found that the browsers embedded in Cursor, GitHub Copilot, Claude Desktop, OpenAI’s Codex and VS Code had been given capabilities that let a web page or local file reach the machine behind them. Cursor’s code-execution issue, disclosed on 10 September, was patched without notice on 1 October, and Copilot’s address-bar spoofing was fixed in version 1.1.29 on 13 October. A local file-read flaw remains unpatched in Codex, reported to OpenAI on 27 July, and in VS Code, which Microsoft treated as falling under its Workspace Trust boundary; OpenAI told The Register it is working on a fix. Neither report lists CVE IDs.

GitHub launches Copilot Fusion, which routes coding work across Anthropic and OpenAI models
Artificial Analysis · The Next Web · 28 October 2026
GitHub launched Copilot Fusion at its Universe conference, a runtime system that orchestrates Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Sonnet 5.5, GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6 Sol. In Artificial Analysis testing, Fusion’s max setting cost $5.59 per task against $11.42 for Claude Code with Opus 5.5 at max, and scored 71 on Terminal-Bench 4.0. GitHub’s Jay Parikh said Fusion Max scored within two points of Opus 5.5 Max on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index, The Next Web reports. GitHub’s Octoverse report says more than 40% of pull requests started with a draft from an agent by September, up from 17% in March.

AI Security
Scan of 7.25 million Gitee repositories finds 26,400 working credentials, including 3,170 DeepSeek keys
The Hacker News · 29 October 2026
Independent researcher Ben Zimmermann, in a post developed with MongoDB’s Kingfisher team, scanned about 7.25 million public repositories on the Chinese code host Gitee and found 26,400 valid credentials, The Hacker News reports. Most were keys for cloud and AI services, led by 9,473 Alibaba Cloud AccessKey pairs, and they include 3,170 DeepSeek keys, 1,348 OpenAI keys and an active organisation-admin credential for a major enterprise AI platform. Zimmermann sent lists to the vendors with the most exposed keys but did not contact the developers who leaked them, and when he published he had not confirmed that any had been revoked.
