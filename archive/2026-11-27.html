Breaking
Microsoft names six threat groups using the ARTEX agentic AI framework in live intrusions
Microsoft Threat Intelligence · 25 November 2026
Microsoft Threat Intelligence says six groups, among them Salt Typhoon, Crystal Typhoon, Onyx Sleet and Pearl Sleet, are using ARTEX, an agentic AI attack framework, for reconnaissance, credential attacks and web application exploitation. The speed is the point: one Storm-0940 campaign logged more than 151,000 failed sign-ins at up to 2,900 a minute, multiple intrusions reached privileged access in under three hours, and one agent went from client secrets on GitHub to Azure Key Vault data in five minutes. ARTEX was posted to GitHub as open source in July and removed in October, but forks remain. Microsoft’s advice is basic hygiene done quickly: phishing-resistant MFA for admins, blocking legacy authentication, rate limiting, and rotating exposed secrets.

Policy & Regulation
Netherlands says it will not accept US MATCH Act limits on ASML exports to China
NL Times · 26 November 2026
Foreign Trade Minister Sjoerd Sjoerdsma told the Dutch parliament on Thursday that “the Netherlands will not be told what to do” and that the MATCH Act’s restrictions go “far, far too far.” The bill, attached to the US defence budget with a vote expected around mid-December, would let Washington impose export rules on its own if allies do not agree joint ones. The government will try to have it removed from the budget and is coordinating with the European Commission; China accounts for about 20% of ASML’s revenue. Sjoerdsma also called it inconsistent that “very advanced AI chips” still reach China while allies are pressed to tighten controls.

Reuters: US chip curbs and a distillation crackdown are delaying China’s next frontier models
Reuters, via Investing.com · 26 November 2026
Z.AI’s GLM-5.5, a model of about 3 trillion parameters planned for November, has not shipped and could slip to the first quarter of 2027 or later, Reuters reports, citing sources. Moonshot AI expects its six-month release cycle could stretch to 12 months or more if its overseas computing access is cut off entirely, which could push Kimi K4 to around mid-2027. The pressure comes from a Commerce rule in force since 19 November that requires licences for overseas data-centre arrangements above roughly 750 H100-equivalent chips, with 90 days for existing operators. According to The Information, Moonshot scrapped a $150 million deal for Blackwell access through an Indonesian data centre.

AI Security
Switzerland confirms five AI-agent-assisted attacks, four of them successful
The Hacker News · 27 November 2026; BleepingComputer · 25 November 2026
Switzerland has confirmed five attacks carried out with help from AI agents, four of which got in; three targeted critical infrastructure operators and two of those succeeded, without major disruption. Earlier this week Switzerland’s NCSC and CSIRT Italia jointly named four frameworks behind such attacks: ARTEX AI, CyberStrikeAI, DeepSeek Harness - Red Team and PentAGI. Italy described a DeepSeek Harness case in which a coordinator tasked five agents with reconnaissance, exploitation and persistence. The NCSC says publicly available AI agents have nearly doubled since October and advises patching critical flaws in internet-facing systems within 24 hours of release.

Chips & Infrastructure
S&P weighs cutting Nvidia’s credit rating to AA- over governance and key-man risk
TechCrunch · 25 November 2026
S&P Global Ratings is considering a one-notch downgrade of Nvidia from AA to AA-, citing “weak executive oversight and lack of succession planning” after Jensen Huang’s ouster and reinstatement. It expects to finish the review within 90 days, and Nvidia would remain investment grade. The rating matters beyond Nvidia: in August the company set out plans to arrange $500 billion in AI infrastructure financing with six institutions, including Goldman Sachs, Apollo and Blackstone, guaranteeing 25% of the loans. A downgrade could raise borrowing costs or collateral demands for the neoclouds it backstops.

A GPT-6 Astra-assisted quantum gravity paper meets a fast rebuttal from Claude referees
The Quantum Insider · 26 November 2026
Stanford physicist Venkatesa Chandrasekaran posted a preprint on 25 November proposing an algebraic quantization of gravity from the null cone, produced with about 400 million tokens of work with OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra on compute OpenAI provided. The paper has not been peer reviewed. A day later physicist Jonathan Oppenheim set a swarm of Claude referees on it: of 21 known obstacles they listed, by his count the paper solves one, sidesteps seven, assumes its way past six and does not mention seven. Oppenheim says he is confident in the overall refutation.

Policy & Regulation
Japan plans binding safety duties and penalties for large AI providers
Seoul Economic Daily, citing the Yomiuri Shimbun · 26 November 2026
Japan would revise its AI promotion law to cover companies with a certain scale of users in Japan, with OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Alibaba and DeepSeek named as likely in scope, according to the Yomiuri report. Providers would have to block answers on cyberattack methods and on making harmful substances or bombs, verify the identity or attributes of users of AI with strong cyberattack capabilities, and report flaws that could be exploited in attacks. Enforcement would escalate from a recommendation to a correction order to penalties, which are still to be worked out. Officials aim to submit the bill to the ordinary Diet session in 2027; compliance with the current law is optional.

Models & Releases
Nvidia opens nearly 19 million synthetic examples and a faster decoder for Nemotron 3.5 Parse
Hugging Face blog (NVIDIA) · 26 November 2026
Nvidia released three synthetic datasets for its Nemotron 3.5 Parse document model: Synthetic Charts V1 (9.98 million examples), Synthetic Tables V1 (1 million) and OCR Synthetic Multilingual v2 (7.9 million). A DFlash2 draft model proposes up to 32 tokens ahead and averages about 14 accepted per step; on one H200 in BF16 it raised throughput 7.62 times at concurrency 1 and 1.52 times at concurrency 512, where it parsed 20.44 pages a second against 13.48 without it. Reinforcement-learning fine-tuning with GRPO lifted a development checkpoint’s overall score from 66.24 to 71.38 and cut looping outputs from 126 pages to 29. The post states no licence for the datasets.
