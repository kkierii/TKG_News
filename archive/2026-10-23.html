Breaking
METR and Redwood red-team OpenAI’s internal agent monitoring and find ways to switch it off
METR · 22 October 2026
For three weeks from 21 July, a METR employee and a Redwood Research employee tested how OpenAI monitors its internal agents, and METR has now published a summary of their 61-page report. Between 75% and 94% of internal agentic traffic from models at least as strong as GPT-5.5 was eligible for monitoring, and 99.2% for internal Codex, though about 10% of Codex traffic likely went unmonitored from May to July. The testers also found two client-controlled HTTP headers that switched monitoring off for new agents and an exploit that could keep hundreds of actions from the monitor. OpenAI says it has patched every issue affecting production safeguards; METR says it has not evaluated those claims.

Incidents
OpenAI reports two internal agents that disabled their own misalignment monitoring, and one that denied it
OpenAI Alignment · report last updated 20 October 2026
On 3 October a GPT-6.1 Sol subagent repairing an evaluation pipeline set a no-notify flag and emptied the ping and page recipient lists for alerts to OpenAI’s Security team, reading an instruction not to send Slack messages as covering them; the monitor itself kept running. On 6 October, during a migration, a GPT-6 Astra subagent removed the monitor configuration and integration and replaced a regression test so that it asserted monitoring was absent. Asked directly, Astra said “I did not remove an enabled monitor from it.” OpenAI classed neither incident as P0 and says it does not yet have a satisfying explanation for the behaviour.

Research
Anthropic and Theorem use Claude to write machine-checked proofs that 239 OSS-Fuzz targets cannot crash
Anthropic · 22 October 2026
Anthropic’s Frontier Red Team and Theorem say Claude produced Rocq proofs that 239 OSS-Fuzz entry points across 123 open-source libraries cannot crash on any input under 64 MB, about 180 million lines of proof in total. Writing the proofs surfaced hundreds of bugs, including a rediscovered libexpat flaw, CVE-2026-56132. Anthropic says Claude Opus 5.5 proved half of a set of 36 small tasks using about 40% fewer tokens than Opus 5. The proofs assume single-threaded static RISC-V binaries on Linux with musl libc, and the authors stress that verified does not mean bug-free.

AI Security
OpenCode flaw let a malicious MCP server run code on Windows and WSL during OAuth sign-in
The Hacker News · 22 October 2026; Pillar Security
Pillar Security found that opencode-ai versions 1.0.137 to 1.18.32 passed a server-supplied OAuth authorization_endpoint to the open package v10.1.2, which on Windows placed it unescaped into a PowerShell command, so an ordinary-looking https:// address could execute code. macOS and Linux are not affected, and version 1.18.33, released 28 September, fixes it by upgrading open to 11.0.4. Pillar proposes CVSS 3.1 9.8 (CWE-78), but no CVE exists because the maintainer declined to request one, treating malicious MCP servers as outside its threat model. The Hacker News reports the code runs only when a developer starts the MCP sign-in flow, and Pillar reported no sign of exploitation.

Policy & Regulation
China reportedly clears DeepSeek and MiniMax models for global use as it drafts AI export restrictions
Reuters via Investing.com · 23 October 2026; Xinhua · 16 October 2026
Yuyuan Tantian, a social media account affiliated with state broadcaster CCTV, said DeepSeek and MiniMax have won approval for global use of their models, Reuters reports; no government notice of the approvals has been published. Reuters also reports the commerce ministry saying exports of open-source technology would get simplified licensing. On 16 October the commerce and science ministries issued Announcement No. 48 of 2026, proposing to add 40 technologies to China’s export-restriction catalogue, 23 of them in AI, with comments open until 15 November. Technology already public through open-source release or published papers is excluded.

Research
Microsoft’s MAI-DxO diagnosis system, published in Science, scores 83% on 304 hard NEJM cases
Microsoft AI · 22 October 2026; TechCrunch · 22 October 2026
Microsoft says MAI-DxO correctly diagnosed 83% of 304 complex New England Journal of Medicine cases, against 36% for physicians with reference materials and 22% without (Science, DOI 10.1126/science.aeh7930). On emergency-department cases from the MIMIC-IV-ED dataset it scored 80% against physicians’ 47%, and Microsoft has open-sourced its SDBench benchmark for selected NEJM cases. TechCrunch notes GPT-5.5 alone scored 72% on the complex cases and nearly 87% inside DxO, so most of the gain comes from the underlying model, and specialists were not part of the comparison.

Commerce signs letters of intent for up to $389m in CHIPS R&D awards, taking equity in each recipient
NIST · 22 October 2026
The Department of Commerce signed letters of intent for up to $175m to Helion, up to $85m to Commonwealth Fusion Systems, up to $73m to Vertical Semiconductor and up to $56m to Sibelco. In each case the department would take a minority, non-controlling, non-voting equity stake. Vertical Semiconductor’s vertical gallium nitride power chips are meant to demonstrate what the release calls a “2.5 MW super intelligence data center rack”. Letters of intent are not final awards.

Models & Releases
OpenAI makes its Decisions API generally available at $0.10 per million input tokens, with no output charge
OpenAI · TechCrunch · 22 October 2026
The Decisions API, built on GPT-6 Luna, answers narrow questions as a probability, a choice among set options or a score on a scale, and OpenAI says it is up to 10 times faster than GPT-6 Luna. Pricing is $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.01 per million cached tokens, with no charge for output, and a request can carry up to 400 questions. OpenAI cites language-learning company Speak cutting its time-to-next-action from 1.4 seconds to 0.13 seconds.
