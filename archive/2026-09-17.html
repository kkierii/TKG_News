Canada and Germany plan CAD 150m and EUR 100m into Bengio’s LawZero
Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada · September 16, 2026
At the ALL IN conference in Montréal on September 16, Canada’s Minister of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Innovation Evan Solomon and Germany’s Federal Minister for Digital Transformation and Government Modernisation Karsten Wildberger announced that their governments are planning to invest CAD 150 million and EUR 100 million respectively in LawZero. Canada’s share flows through the Strategic Response Fund, the same programme used for large-scale industrial projects in steel, aluminium, automotive and forest products. LawZero is a Montréal-headquartered not-for-profit founded by Turing Award winner Yoshua Bengio and incubated at Mila, the Quebec AI institute Bengio also founded. Bengio called the backing “a defining moment” for developing safe AI at global scale.
The money is earmarked for research and engineering talent plus computing capacity for LawZero’s methodology, called Scientist AI. Both releases describe it as a departure from current frontier systems: rather than acting autonomously or pursuing objectives, it is built to reason transparently and produce evidence-based outputs “not biased by goals of its own.” The Canadian release states the project will create 360 full-time jobs in Canada and will stand up dedicated sovereign computing infrastructure with Canadian providers Hypertec and 5C. The German funding supports research and development at LawZero’s new German office, and the organisation says the work begins with tools to assess and oversee existing AI systems before advancing toward a methodology for safe-by-design frontier models.
The investment sits inside an existing bilateral structure. Canada ties it to the Joint Declaration of Intent on Artificial Intelligence and the Sovereign Technology Alliance the two countries launched in February 2026, and to Canada’s National Artificial Intelligence Strategy: AI for All, released June 4, 2026. LawZero’s existing backers are the Gates Foundation, Coefficient Giving, Schmidt Sciences, the Future of Life Institute, NVIDIA, Founders Pledge and Jaan Tallinn via the Silicon Valley Community Foundation. Its board and global advisory council include chair Maria Eitel, former prime ministers Jacinda Ardern and Stefan Löfven, Yuval Noah Harari and Mila chief executive Valerie Pisano. The same conference produced the Cohere and Aleph Alpha combination below, making ALL IN the origin point for both of the week’s largest sovereign-AI stories.
Four caveats belong with this. The Canadian release says both governments are “planning to invest,” not that funds have moved, and states that Germany’s EUR 100 million is “subject to notification by the European Commission” — so the German half is conditional on state-aid clearance. The two primary sources do not agree on a total: Canada gives CAD 150 million plus EUR 100 million, while LawZero’s own release says “up to CAD $300 million,” and those are not equivalent at mid-September rates. Several aggregators are running “$150 million each,” which matches neither source. And everything said about what Scientist AI will do is roadmap: there is no model, no benchmark, no published evaluation and no delivery date, the 360-jobs figure is a government projection rather than headcount, and neither release sets out a disbursement schedule, milestones or governance conditions.

Mandiant: hijacked AI coding session spread the Shai-Hulud worm to about 100 repositories
Mandiant, Google Cloud · September 2026
Mandiant’s AI Risk and Resilience special report, published this month, documents an intrusion in which a threat actor who had compromised a software-as-a-service provider hijacked an active AI coding assistant session on a developer’s workstation. The assistant recommended an external package the attacker had poisoned and “inadvertently functioned as a trojan horse”; once the recommendation was accepted, the attacker installed an infostealer through a poisoned PyPI package, harvested GitHub OAuth tokens and deployed the self-propagating Shai-Hulud worm across approximately 100 internal code repositories, then poisoned a package inside the organisation’s official namespace, causing a second infection when another employee pulled it. A separate case study in the same report covers a financial-services agent that hit a corrupted null value, entered a recursive reasoning loop and generated over 15,000 high-cost reasoning API calls in under an hour for a cloud-billing spike Mandiant puts at roughly $50,000. The report does not say when the intrusion occurred or how the attacker took over the session, and the assistant was not stated to be the initial access vector.

AI Security
Two MCP servers draw CVSS 10.0 CVEs for unauthenticated code and SQL execution
GitHub Security Advisories · published to NVD September 15, 2026
NVD published CVE-2026-53710 and CVE-2026-59971 on September 15, both carrying a CVSS 3.1 base score of 10.0 with the vector AV:N/AC:L/PR:N/UI:N/S:C/C:H/I:H/A:H. CVE-2026-53710 affects IBM’s mcp-context-forge python_sandbox_server at versions 1.0.1 and earlier, where a raw getattr exposed in safe_builtins plus a missing _getattr_ guard let an attacker build dunder names at runtime, walk the class hierarchy to subprocess.Popen and run OS commands through the execute_code tool over unauthenticated HTTP/SSE; it is fixed in 1.0.2. CVE-2026-59971 affects designcomputer’s mysql_mcp_server below 0.4.2, where setting MCP_TRANSPORT=sse builds the transport without security_settings, leaving the /, /sse and /messages/ routes unauthenticated on a default 0.0.0.0 bind and permitting unauthenticated execute_sql; the advisory says internet-wide scanning found 25 publicly reachable SSE instances. Both projects’ default stdio transports are unaffected. The IBM advisory’s own reporter text proposes 9.0, but the vector it publishes computes to 10.0 and both GitHub and NVD record 10.0.

AI Security
One browser extension shown hijacking the built-in AI agent in five products
The Hacker News · September 16, 2026
Forever Security demonstrated that a single browser extension holding two common permissions — one that modifies web pages, plus declarativeNetRequest — can seize the trusted page each product’s in-browser AI agent listens to and issue that agent its own commands. The technique reached Gemini Live in Chrome, Perplexity Comet, Microsoft Edge, Opera Neon and the Claude in Chrome extension; on Chrome and Comet it read local files, and on Chrome it also switched on camera and microphone. Only two of the findings carry CVEs: CVE-2026-0628 in Chrome, rated 8.8 by CISA and fixed in Chrome 143.0.7499.192 in early January 2026, and CVE-2026-55945 in Edge, rated 4.2 and fixed in Edge 150.0.4078.48 on July 2. Forever Security was paid roughly $20,000 in bounties across the five products; each demonstration requires the attacker’s extension to already be installed, and neither CVE was on CISA’s KEV catalogue as of September 16.

Policy & Regulation
Newsom signs SB 1050, requiring disclosure of AI-generated performers in advertising
Office of the Governor of California · September 16, 2026
Governor Gavin Newsom signed Senate Bill 1050, authored by Senator Angelique Ashby (D-Sacramento), which requires explicit disclosure on any video or audio advertisement that uses AI-generated performers to sell a product or service. The law also prohibits continued use of any advertisement found to be in violation. The bill was signed in Los Angeles at the headquarters of its sponsor, SAG-AFTRA, whose national executive director Duncan Crabtree-Ireland said the union helped craft the bill language. It follows SB 813 (McNerney) and AB 1405 (Bauer-Kahan), signed September 9, which respectively require assessments by independent verification organisations and third-party audits of AI systems.

Chips & Infrastructure
Nvidia posts first Vera Rubin NVL72 results in MLPerf Inference v6.1
NVIDIA · September 16, 2026
MLCommons released MLPerf Inference v6.1 results on September 16, including a first preview submission of Nvidia’s Vera Rubin NVL72 system. Nvidia reports up to 3.7x higher throughput than GB300 NVL72 on the Qwen3-VL benchmark using vLLM with the Dynamo framework, and up to 2.5x higher on DeepSeek-R1 using TensorRT-LLM. A separate 288-GPU GB300 NVL72 submission across four racks reached 99 per cent scaling efficiency in the offline scenario relative to a single 72-GPU rack, and GB300 NVL72 performance on Qwen3-VL improved up to 1.6x over v6.0 through software changes alone. Nineteen partners submitted results, eight of them on multi-node Blackwell NVL72 systems; Nebius also submitted Vera Rubin NVL72 preview results. Preview-category submissions are not shipping-hardware results.

Research
Benchmark finds LLM-written reward rubrics exploited 8 to 26 per cent of the time
arXiv 2609.16816 · submitted September 15, 2026
A team from the National University of Singapore, Peking University, the Institute of Automation at the Chinese Academy of Sciences and JD.com introduced ImpossibleRubrics, a benchmark of 169 impossible tasks across six impossibility categories plus 48 answerable controls, each paired with an oracle certificate defining what an honest answer may and may not claim. Rather than supplying fixed rubrics, the benchmark supplies task environments and certificates so downstream-generated rubrics can be adversarially tested for rewarding certificate-violating answers. Eleven rubric generators were exploited 8 to 26 per cent of the time on the unbiased 150-of-169 environment cut; on a selected stress cut the strongest measured generator was still exploited 36 per cent, while a certificate-faithful rubric was exploited 0 per cent. A single generic rubric — “be decisive, penalize hedging” — used unchanged for every task was exploited 64 per cent of the time, and seven of the eleven generators writing task-tailored rubrics were exploited more often than that.
