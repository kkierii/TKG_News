Binance says a fake job interview and an abused internal AI tool led to its $6.43 billion theft
The Hacker News, citing Binance · 3 November 2026
Binance said on Tuesday that the record $6.43 billion theft from the exchange followed a multi-stage campaign in which an attacker posing as a recruiter had a Binance engineer download a malicious interview “assignment” in September. On 20 October the attacker used that engineer’s compromised computer and an internal AI tool to take control of two colleagues’ access, then ran the attack on 29 October between 17:15 and 19:56. Chainalysis ties the method to North Korea-linked fake-interview schemes, which it says caused more than $14.3 million in losses over the past 18 months. Binance says it has rebuilt the affected wallet system in a new environment.

AI Security
Google says LLMjacking attackers ran up as much as $1 million in AI cloud charges at some organisations
Google Threat Intelligence Group · 2 November 2026
Google Threat Intelligence Group and Mandiant report that attackers are stealing cloud credentials and model API keys to resell access to large language models, with fraudulent charges above $100,000 in a single day in individual incidents and cumulative charges reaching $1 million at some organisations before access was revoked. One group, UNC6993, hosted a fake application portal that tricked users into handing over session tokens. Another actor exploited CVE-2026-40217, a user ID rebind flaw in LiteLLM’s /key/regenerate endpoint, to turn ordinary virtual keys into PROXY_ADMIN credentials. Google says a routing service called Jet Night, which offers an unlimited plan for about $60 a month, passed nearly $1 million in token costs to one victim.

AMD reports record $13.4 billion quarter as data-centre revenue nearly doubles
AMD · 3 November 2026
AMD reported third-quarter revenue of $13.4 billion, up 45% year over year, with record data-centre revenue of $8.5 billion, up 97%. It credited demand for EPYC processors and the launch of its Instinct MI450 Series GPUs. Non-GAAP earnings per share were $2.09, up 74%, while client and gaming revenue fell 8% to $3.7 billion. AMD guided fourth-quarter revenue to about $17 billion, plus or minus 3%, which would be about 66% growth at the midpoint, as it scales Helios rack systems and “Venice” production.

AI Security
OpenAI raises bug bounties to as much as $172,000 and brings in Cathedral for a security sprint
The Decoder; Gizmodo · 3 November 2026
OpenAI now pays up to $30,000 for critical vulnerabilities in ChatGPT or Codex and up to $100,000 for exceptional findings, and bonuses can lift the total to about $172,000, The Decoder reports. It is also preparing a security sprint in which the firm Cathedral will work with OpenAI engineers to stress-test its systems; in the previous sprint, 25% of its product engineers hunted for vulnerabilities. Cathedral, founded by former DOGE staff, raised $160 million at a reported $1.4 billion valuation and is best known for breaking into Anthropic’s systems through Anthropic’s bug bounty programme. Anthropic’s Logan Graham said that intrusion took “several days” and did not reach model weights or Claude conversations.

Policy & Regulation
At least 14 Ohio municipalities vote for data-centre bans or restrictions
Fortune · 4 November 2026
Voters in 18 Ohio municipalities decided 21 data-centre ballot measures on Tuesday, more than half of the 41 on ballots nationwide, and at least 14 municipalities passed bans or other restrictions, according to unofficial results. In Trenton, a ban passed 80% to 20%; in Perry, more than 87% backed a measure requiring developers who seek tax breaks to give the village financial guarantees. In Wilmington, around 80% of voters rejected rezoning for a data-centre project across four referendums. Ohio already has 125 local data-centre moratoriums.

Research
Anthropic releases FlashProve, which proves a response came from specific model weights
Anthropic · 2 November 2026
Anthropic’s Frontier Red Team, with researchers from EPFL and MIT, published FlashProve, a zkSNARK-based system that proves a response was generated by a committed set of model weights without revealing them. On Llama 4 Maverick, a 400B-parameter mixture-of-experts model, a single H100 produces proofs at around 110 tokens per second, with a proof of about 19 MB per 1,024-token request. Proving takes roughly 70 to 95 times the GPU time of serving, and the proofs cover greedy decoding only. The code, Lean proofs and documentation are released under the Apache 2.0 licence as intermediate research.

Research
Stanford study finds 44% of 50 widely cited findings about LLMs no longer hold
Stanford University (Zou Group) · 2 November 2026
Stanford researchers retested 50 widely cited findings about large language models on 20 models released between July 2024 and September 2026 from eight developers. They found 44% of the claims have expired, 34% have weakened significantly, 16% persist and 6% have strengthened. Among expired claims with an estimated date, the time from publication to expiry averaged 15.7 months (median 14.7). In one reproduction, GPT-3.5 Turbo fabricated 59% of 210 references, close to the original paper’s 55%.
