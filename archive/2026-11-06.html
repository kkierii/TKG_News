Democrats plan bills on AI testing, liability and data-centre power costs when they take over Congress

AI Security
Anthropic leaves a Claude Dispatch flaw open that lets a browser extension send commands to Claude Desktop
The Register · 5 November 2026
Researcher Gal Weizman found that a malicious browser extension with control of claude.ai can use Dispatch to send commands to Claude Desktop, including on a different machine signed into the same account. Anthropic accepted the report, dubbed ThisBash, as valid in May but closed it as a duplicate, with no bounty and no CVE, and told him Dispatch would be retired at the end of October. As of 30 October Claude’s support page still listed Dispatch in limited beta. Weizman says the weakness also exists in Claude Code Remote Control and Cowork sessions.

Models & Releases
OpenAI releases GPT-6 Cyber to vetted Daybreak Red customers, rated Critical for cybersecurity
OpenAI system card · 5 November 2026
GPT-6 Cyber is a GPT-6 Astra variant for authorised vulnerability research, available only through Daybreak Red, which requires identity verification and at least one FIDO2 security key. OpenAI reports a 48.45% intended-exploit success rate on ExploitGym, against 42.43% for GPT-6 Astra. In a test where exploiting a planted service was out of scope, it did so in 0.07% of runs without safety instructions and 0% with them. OpenAI rates it Critical in cybersecurity, the same designation as Astra; the figures are OpenAI’s own.

Research
METR estimates Anthropic is now making AI research progress about twice as fast as before
METR · 5 November 2026
After eleven weeks of independent access from 12 August, METR estimates Anthropic is improving its models on well-specified tasks at around twice the rate it did between March 2024 and October 2025, and would not be surprised if the true figure were nearer 1.3x or 3x. It guesses that perhaps 80% of the current acceleration is due to Anthropic’s use of AI in its own research. METR says Anthropic redacted a majority of the evidence it found most informative, so readers must rely on its judgment.

AI Security
Check Point bypassed ChatGPT Lockdown Mode to exfiltrate chats without user interaction
The Hacker News · 5 November 2026
Check Point found that ChatGPT’s HTML previews in code blocks used nested sandboxed frames whose permissions still allowed popups, defeating Lockdown Mode’s block on outbound requests. Lockdown Mode, introduced in June 2026, is an optional setting meant to limit data exfiltration through prompt injection. Check Point says the flaw allowed silent chat theft, phishing and malware delivery, including zero-click exfiltration in the ChatGPT desktop app. OpenAI fixed it on 18 September after responsible disclosure.

Policy & Regulation
China blocks SoftBank’s $5.4 billion purchase of ABB’s robotics division over AI training data
Reuters, via WHTC · 5 November 2026
China’s National Development and Reform Commission told SoftBank it will not allow the $5.4 billion takeover, according to the Financial Times. The FT reported that the NDRC was concerned robotics data could be transferred overseas and used to train AI models, including OpenAI’s, in which SoftBank is a major investor. It is the second major deal China has blocked this year, after Meta was ordered to unwind its $2-billion-plus purchase of Manus. SoftBank shares ended the day down 6.8%.

Chips & Infrastructure
Google commits $17 billion to its first Wyoming data centre, with a 702 MW fuel-cell plant
Google · 5 November 2026
Google is building its first Wyoming data centre in Laramie County with partners Tallgrass Energy, Black Hills Energy, American Electric Power and Related Digital. The project is contracted with AEP for a 702 MW fuel-cell development with carbon capture and storage, which Google says brings its contracted CCS capacity to nearly 1 gigawatt. Google says it will pay for 100% of the power it uses, and the site will be air-cooled to limit water use. The figures are Google’s own.

Incidents
Acronis traces an attack campaign on 11 countries to one operator running 12 AI agents
Cyber Security News, on Acronis research · 5 November 2026
Acronis found the campaign through what looked like a misconfigured development server holding more than 90,000 files and about 9GB of logs, and tied it to one person in Hualien City, Taiwan. The operator ran 12 AI agents, nine of them active, against emergency services, banks, universities and military systems. The agents collected about 206,800 residential phone records in Mexico and found 489 targets across 45 Thai universities. More than half of one tool’s high-severity findings were false positives.
