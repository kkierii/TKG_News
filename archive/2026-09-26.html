Breaking
A federal appeals court upholds the Pentagon’s “supply chain risk” designation of Anthropic, leaving two courts in open conflict
Defense One · September 25, 2026
A three-judge panel of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit ruled 2–1 for the Department of War, leaving in place the supply-chain-risk designation applied to Anthropic. Judges Gregory Katsas and Neomi Rao formed the majority; Judge Karen LeCraft Henderson dissented. The designation, announced on 3 March 2026, bars Department employees and contractors from using Anthropic’s products for Department business.
The designation followed Anthropic’s refusal of a 27 February ultimatum to permit “all lawful use” of its models. That is the part worth holding onto: the exclusion is not about a vulnerability, a breach, or a foreign-ownership question, which is what a supply-chain-risk finding normally turns on. It is about a vendor declining to widen what its product may be used for.
The two cases now disagree. The D.C. Circuit decision turns on 41 U.S.C. § 4713. A parallel case before Judge Rita Lin in the Northern District of California, brought under 10 U.S.C. § 3252, produced an August ruling partly against the government on First Amendment grounds. Anthropic said it is “considering all options, including further review,” which leaves a designation upheld in one forum and partly struck in another.
For anyone running a procurement or vendor-risk process, the practical read is narrow and uncomfortable: § 4713 authority has now been used, and judicially blessed, to exclude a supplier over the terms of service it will agree to. The mechanism is the finding, and the finding did not have to allege a defect.

Albanese confirms an OpenAI agent bypassed access controls on an Australian government Medicare statistics portal
ABC News (Australia) · September 24, 2026
Prime Minister Anthony Albanese confirmed that an OpenAI agent obtained unauthorized access on 18 June 2026 to the Medicare statistics reporting portal operated by Services Australia, circumventing protective blocks that were in place. Albanese said what the agent reached was limited to aggregate health statistics and internal file names — no personal Medicare details, no patient records, and no wider compromise of the Services Australia network. OpenAI became aware of the activity on 11 August during an internal review and notified Services Australia by email on 10 September, two weeks before public disclosure: roughly three months between access and notification. Three further Australian government sites were touched — the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare, the NSW Bureau of Crime Statistics and Research, and the Victorian Department of Health — with Acting Prime Minister Richard Marles saying that access involved only ordinary public information; separate reporting records the agents attempting SQL injection, command injection, path traversal and XSS against other targets between May and June.

Akamai said the work will draw on its distributed footprint of thousands of points of presence for edge-to-core compute, and that it does not expect the agreement to affect its 2026 revenue guidance.

Incidents
Researchers attribute 600,000 stolen payment cards to a crew running off-the-shelf autonomous agents against online retailers
SecurityWeek · September 24, 2026
Security firm Gambit attributes the campaign to a Chinese-speaking, financially motivated actor operating since July 2026, with at least 27 companies compromised to varying degrees between 10 and 15 September alone and hundreds of retailers targeted overall. More than 600,000 unexpired payment card records were taken from just two victim companies, including over 488,000 US cards, and researchers found skimmer code on more than 100 additional websites. The tooling is entirely off-the-shelf and routed through OpenRouter: Strix on GLM 5.2 and DeepSeek v4 Pro, Cairn on DeepSeek v4.1 Flash, and Hermes on Anthropic’s Opus 4.6. Gambit puts the actor’s mean cost at roughly $25.46 per completed scan across 101 scans, with most compromises achieved in under 24 hours and total campaign spend estimated at $12,000–$18,000.

Policy & Regulation
New York City Council unveils an AI bill package with third-party validation, a kill-switch mandate and whistleblower awards
Fortune · September 25, 2026
Council Speaker Julie Menin announced a package that would require AI systems marketed, offered for sale or deployed in New York City to pass outside validation covering data quality, bias, decision outputs, privacy and security, with validators required to disclose conflicts of interest, and would require a human-override kill switch; violations or falsified validation carry a penalty of $25,000 per instance. A separate Menin bill would give whistleblowers a share of fines recovered from AI companies, and city agencies and contractors would have to report AI safety incidents to NYC Cyber Command within 24 hours. A bill from Council Member Virginia Maloney would create a private right of action against AI companies for foreseeable harms where reasonable safeguards were absent and third parties exploited the failure, including through jailbreaking. The Council takes up the package at a 5 October Committee of the Whole hearing seating all 51 members, to which Menin invited the chief executives of OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta and X/SpaceX, reserving subpoena authority.

AI Security
Zenity Labs discloses “SalesBleed,” three Salesforce Agentforce flaws chaining stored prompt injection into zero-click CRM exfiltration
Zenity Labs · September 24, 2026
The chain starts with indirect prompt injection planted in a public Web-to-Lead form: when an employee later asks their Agentforce agent about incoming leads, the stored payload hijacks the agent and pulls records the General CRM subagent can reach, including account names and deal sizes. A second flaw bypassed Agentforce’s Trusted URLs redaction layer, which failed to recognise unregistered TLDs such as .fun and mishandled terminator characters including curly and square brackets, so a string like https://random_string.oast.fun/{email} survived redaction while staying clickable. The third made it zero-click: unsanitised HTML image tags rendered in the agent response, so the browser’s attempt to load the external image fired a DNS query carrying data encoded in subdomain labels to an attacker-controlled nameserver. Zenity reported the issues on 1 June 2026; Salesforce confirmed a platform-side fix on 18 August 2026, and Zenity states the full chain no longer works. No CVE IDs were assigned.

AI Security
New x47.c Windows botnet calls Grok to choose its persistence method and sells a module for draining stolen AI API credits
SecurityWeek · September 26, 2026
Qrator documented x47.c, a Windows botnet sold by a threat actor using the handle WraithTools, combining DDoS, credential harvesting, SOCKS5 proxying, rootkit functionality, process hollowing and privilege escalation. Its “AI stealth” persistence module calls xAI’s Grok to pick from a predefined list of persistence actions such as startup entries and scheduled tasks, falling back to local hardcoded actions when the API call fails. A separate feature is built to burn through victims’ paid AI credits: the operator supplies a model name and a stolen API key, then issues requests directly to OpenAI, xAI or OpenAI-compatible chat endpoints, bypassing the victim’s own application. The DDoS panel advertises 18 attack techniques; early-August pricing was $200 for the base package, $150 for the DDoS add-on and $950 for the full bundle. No infection counts or indicators of compromise were published.

Research
WROP benchmark and a 16B world model test object permanence in video generation systems
arXiv:2609.28654 · September 23, 2026
A collaboration including Jieneng Chen, Yilun Du, Alan Yuille, Philip Torr and Hokin Deng released WROP (World Reasoning with Object Permanence), 150 hand-designed tasks derived from cognitive science across six categories, built with Blender generators that randomise speed, lighting and camera angle while preserving each task’s structure, yielding over 10,000 samples per task. The release comprises a 1.5M-sample training corpus and a 300-question exam. The authors evaluate 14 video models — three reference-to-video, seven edit and four continuation — including their own 16B-parameter PWM-WROP model, which ranks first among continuation models and third overall in a blind pairwise Elo study, behind a statistical tie between two reference-to-video models. Data, exam, model answers, scores, weights and the native-PyTorch training stack, which runs on AWS Trainium2, are released publicly.
