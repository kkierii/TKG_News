Breaking
Amodei calls for pacing the AI frontier; three rival chief executives back him within nine hours, one calls it a cartel
darioamodei.com · September 12, 2026
Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei published a roughly 3,800-word essay, “We Must Pace the Frontier,” on Saturday morning, proposing that frontier AI developers slow the rate of capability gains without halting model training. The piece sets out three steps: embedded third-party evaluators, coordination among labs in democracies, and global coordination. It contains no compute thresholds, no numeric capability thresholds and no timeline for the pacing itself; Amodei writes that pacing “does not mean halting model training or technical progress.”
The one unilateral commitment is the first. Anthropic says it will give outside evaluators — it names METR — desks in its offices, access badges, company laptops and permissions comparable to internal risk teams, under a contract letting them publish findings without Anthropic’s editorial control. Redactions are limited to security-sensitive, legally privileged, commercially sensitive or third-party confidential material, and reviewers may state publicly if a redaction removed something important. The essay estimates six to twelve months before an agent swarm could run a persistent internet-wide botnet causing hundreds of billions of dollars in damage, and argues pacing would buy one to two years of interpretability and evaluations progress.
Three rival chief executives responded the same day. Sam Altman of OpenAI wrote that he agreed and that OpenAI “will do the same” on independent evaluators with employee-like access — the only matching commitment. Elon Musk of SpaceXAI posted “Dario is right.” Demis Hassabis of Google DeepMind said the essay “points towards the right path forward” while redirecting to an industry-wide standards body. Satya Nadella of Microsoft welcomed the evaluator proposal on Sunday, with the qualifier that this “cannot be controlled by a handful of entities.” Meta did not respond publicly.
Cohere chief executive Aidan Gomez dissented on Sunday in an essay subtitled “AI Needs Evidenced Standards, Not A Cartel,” arguing the proposal’s request for antitrust waivers is “a cartel by any other name” and noting the essay promises pacing without sacrificing commercial advantage. China’s foreign ministry spokesman Guo Jiakun called the episode “fear mongering” on Monday. Equities fell as the week opened: CNN reported Nasdaq futures down 1.5 per cent, SoftBank closing nearly 11 per cent lower in Japan, the Kospi down 3.3 per cent, SK hynix down 6.4 per cent and ASML down 6 per cent.
Two caveats belong with this story. Anthropic is preparing a Nasdaq listing, filed confidentially on June 1 and reported to target a valuation near $2tn, which several critics raised directly. And a separate “Pacing the Frontier” open letter signed by more than 1,000 frontier-lab employees was published on July 28; it is a different document, and some aggregators are blending the two.

Policy & Regulation
House Speaker says Congress will not lead on AI safety rules and rejects an emergency moratorium
Axios · September 13, 2026
Speaker Mike Johnson said on CNN’s “State of the Union” and NBC’s “Meet the Press” on Sunday that lawmakers must “resist Congress jumping in and imposing some sort of emergency moratorium,” adding that it “has to be done right, safely, wisely.” He had been urged earlier in the week to cancel the House recess and recall members to pass AI safeguards, and said he would do so and schedule a vote “if we had a solution.” Johnson proposed instead convening President Trump, members of Congress and the heads of the major AI labs, saying the companies “all have very different ideas on what the guardrails should be” and that there is no consensus among them. The remarks followed Amodei’s Saturday essay.

AI Security
Anthropic says an operator used Claude to scan 1.8 million Android apps for hardcoded secrets
Cyber Security News · September 14, 2026
Anthropic’s September 2026 threat intelligence report describes a French-speaking operator using the aliases MeowSHA, frkoo and blazespider who ran ten AWS EC2 workers that downloaded, decompiled and scanned 1.8 million distinct Android APKs with TruffleHog for hardcoded API keys, cloud credentials and tokens, routing verified hits to Telegram channels organised into more than 100 source categories. A parallel stream harvested exposed GitHub personal access tokens from organisation email addresses, and together the two pipelines supplied initial access for several confirmed intrusions. In one supply-chain incident the actors extracted data from roughly 200 downstream customer organisations and dumped more than 2,100 Azure AD token sets across over 40 corporate tenants in about 34 hours. Anthropic said one stolen AI API key was reused for roughly three weeks against other organisations, that the keys came from customer environments, and that its own systems were not compromised.

Research
AI-generated patches fully resolved 26 per cent of tested vulnerabilities, and broke things more often than that
The Register · September 13, 2026
1Password’s research team took six CVEs disclosed since March 2026 and generated 6,080 candidate patches using OpenAI’s ChatGPT-5.5 and Anthropic’s Opus 4.8. Director of security research Keith Hoodlet reported a mean 26.0 per cent success rate for patches that fully resolved the flaw without materially changing application behaviour, while 53.9 per cent either failed to fix the vulnerability, introduced a new one, or both; 20 per cent of working patches still altered application behaviour. A separate Veracode study across more than 100 models and 80 coding tasks found an average security pass rate of 56 per cent for AI-generated code. Trend Micro ZDI’s Dustin Childs noted that Microsoft’s September 2026 Patch Tuesday addressed 974 CVEs.

Chips & Infrastructure
Samsung and SK hynix reject Kepco’s 25tn won electricity prepayment plan
Korea JoongAng Daily · September 14, 2026
Samsung Electronics and SK hynix have turned down a Korea Electric Power Corp proposal that they advance a combined 25 trillion won ($18.6bn) to fund transmission and substation construction for semiconductor clusters, with Kepco confirming both rejections. Kepco had asked Samsung for roughly 20 trillion won and SK hynix for roughly 5 trillion won — about five times each company’s 2025 electricity bills — offering interest above the two-year government bond yield and repayment through deductions from power bills every six months. Both chipmakers concluded a five-year cash commitment was too risky given uncertainty over how long the memory upcycle will last and their need to fund fab expansion and R&D. Kepco’s total debt stood at 210.7 trillion won at the end of June, with interest expense of about 11.5 billion won a day.

Policy & Regulation
UK parliamentary committee calls for an AI Bill and a single statutory AI regulator
UK Parliament, Joint Committee on Human Rights · September 14, 2026
The Joint Committee on Human Rights published its 4th Report of the session, “Human Rights and the Regulation of AI” (HC 160 / HL Paper 56), on Monday, concluding that the UK framework is fragmented and applies mainly at the point of deployment rather than design. It recommends a risk-tiered AI Bill imposing graded due-diligence and transparency duties across the AI supply chain, outright prohibition of some uses — naming subliminal techniques and inappropriate profiling or biometric use — subject to public consultation, and prior approval for high-risk systems. The report also calls for a single independent statutory oversight body able to set codes of practice and sanction wrongdoing, and for stronger UK GDPR rules on automated decision-making, stating that a human in the loop alone is not meaningful human involvement. Chair Alex Sobel MP noted the AI Security Institute engages developers voluntarily and has no statutory power.

Anthropic bans accounts in Houthi-held northern Yemen that used Claude Code for missile guidance work
SecurityWeek · September 12, 2026
In the same September threat report, Anthropic said it identified and banned accounts run by a cell in mountainous northern Yemen pursuing three separate weapons programmes, including a multi-variant missile intended to glide at hypersonic speed and a warhead using mobile phone hardware for mid-course manoeuvring. The company said the operators used Claude Code rather than human software engineers to develop guidance, navigation and control software. No operational device was fielded, but the group conducted a failed guided-rocket test — which Anthropic knows because the users returned to Claude to ask why it failed — and had already built an offline simulation toolkit that does not depend on Claude or any other platform before the accounts were banned. The Houthi political bureau, through Hazam al-Assad, denied the account.

Models & Releases
InternLM releases Intern-S2-397B, a 403B-parameter Apache-2.0 multimodal mixture-of-experts model
InternLM / Shanghai AI Laboratory · September 13, 2026
The repository was created on September 13 and carries 403,423,094,768 BF16 parameters across 188 safetensors shards under an Apache-2.0 licence. Its configuration specifies a mixture-of-experts architecture with 60 layers, 512 experts and 10 active per token, hidden size 4,096, 32 attention heads, a 251,392-token vocabulary and maximum position embeddings of 262,144; a 27-layer SigLIP-style vision tower handles image input, and the chat template also accepts a time-series content type. The model card says text reasoning benchmarks were run at a 256K maximum inference length and multimodal benchmarks at 64K, with thinking mode on by default. Deployment is documented for LMDeploy, vLLM and SGLang.

Research
Expert audit finds leading physics benchmarks substantially understate frontier model scores
arXiv · announced September 14, 2026
A 50-author team led from Yale re-evaluated frontier models on six widely used physics benchmarks, having faculty and graduate researchers review problem statements, reference solutions and model responses to separate genuine model errors from grader errors, wrong reference solutions and underspecified questions. After experts corrected erroneous reference solutions and repaired or excluded flawed questions, GPT-5.6-Sol’s mean@4 rose from 47.3 per cent to 78.7 per cent on HLE-Physics and from 61.0 per cent to 87.2 per cent on CMT-Benchmark, with corrected pass@4 reaching 94.4 per cent on the 54 retained CritPt challenges. Scores on audited subsets of UGPhysics, PRISM-Physics and PHYBench also rose. The authors state that most audited cases initially marked incorrect reflected benchmarking problems rather than physics reasoning errors. The paper is a preprint, submitted September 11 and announced Monday, and has not been peer-reviewed.
