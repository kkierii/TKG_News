OpenAI Says Astra Is the First Model It Has Designated Critical for Cybersecurity Under Its Preparedness Framework
OpenAI, “Path to Astra: critical capabilities and frontier safeguards,” 1 September 2026
OpenAI published “Path to Astra” on 1 September 2026, stating that it now believes the forthcoming Astra model meets the Critical cybersecurity capability threshold under its Preparedness Framework. It is the first model OpenAI has designated at that level. The Framework sets Critical at either of two conditions — that the model can identify and develop functional zero-day exploits of all severity levels in many hardened real-world critical systems without human intervention, or that it can devise and execute end-to-end novel attack strategies against hardened targets given only a high-level goal. In expert-led assessments OpenAI says Astra found previously unknown vulnerabilities and turned them into working exploit chains: a full browser-compromise chain that escaped the sandbox and executed commands on the host when the browser opened an HTML file, and multiple flaws in a hardened operating system combined into a local privilege-escalation chain from an unprivileged user to root. On an internal benchmark built from 20 recently disclosed high-severity V8 vulnerabilities, the model discovered and used two zero-days as part of an exploit chain, which OpenAI says it is in the process of disclosing to the maintainers. Two qualifications sit in the post’s own fine print and are easy to lose in summary: the capability results shown reflect Astra with Daybreak Blue access rather than the default production configuration, and the honeypot comparison — GPT‑5.6 Sol attempting to reach out-of-scope targets in 56% of runs where Astra attempted none — was measured with safeguards in observation-only mode, which OpenAI states is not normal production use. On the safeguards side the company reports Astra refusing 91.5% of requests on its cyber jailbreak evaluation set against 59% for GPT‑5.6 Sol, says it restarted on 28 August the large frontier reinforcement-learning run it had paused after the Hugging Face incident, and says advanced cybersecurity workflows will reach only a small group of alpha testers first, with wider defensive access following through Daybreak Blue.

Anthropic Ships Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1, Holding Token Prices and Cutting Cache Reads 75%
Anthropic announcement, 1 September 2026; AWS What’s New, 1 September 2026
Anthropic announced Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1 on 1 September 2026 — the same underlying model shipped at two safeguard levels, with Fable 5.1 generally available under the API model ID claude-fable-5-1 and Mythos 5.1 restricted to vetted participants in the company’s Cyber Verification and Life Sciences Verification Programs. Per-token pricing is unchanged at $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output, but cache reads fall 75% to $0.25 per million, which Anthropic estimates cuts typical workload cost by about 25% and highly agentic workloads by up to roughly 45%. On benchmarks the company reports Fable 5.1 at 52.6% on Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 against 24.7% for Fable 5 and 29.0% for Opus 5, 55.8% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 with Mythos 5.1 at 60.9%, 73.4% on CursorBench 3.2.0 and 60.9% on Humanity’s Last Exam without tools. The models are available on Amazon Bedrock and Claude Platform on AWS, Google Cloud and Microsoft Foundry; all benchmark figures are Anthropic’s own and are not independently reproduced.

California’s Legislature Adjourned Having Passed 30 AI Bills; Newsom Has Until 30 September to Act
California legislative record via LegiScan; Transparency Coalition session tally, 31 August 2026
California’s Legislature met its midnight deadline on Monday 31 August 2026 and closed the 2025–26 session having passed 30 AI-related bills, 28 of which go to Governor Gavin Newsom, who has until 30 September to sign or veto. Measures enrolled on the final day include SB 947 by Senator Jerry McNerney, limiting employer use of automated decision systems, on a 28–10 Senate concurrence vote; SB 903 by Senator Steve Padilla, restricting AI from providing mental-health therapy, 40–0; SB 867, also Padilla, banning companion chatbots in toys, 39–0; and AB 1709 by Assembly Member Josh Lowenthal on addictive social-media features for under-16s, which cleared the Senate 39–0. The marquee measure is SB 1119, carried by Padilla with Assembly Members Buffy Wicks and Rebecca Bauer-Kahan, which would require companion-chatbot operators to determine user age, provide parental controls, undergo independent audits and limit targeted advertising to minors; OpenAI published a letter from Ann O’Leary, its VP of Global Policy, the same day urging Newsom to sign it. One status caveat worth carrying: SB 1119 was amended in the Assembly and the official history’s last recorded action is “Read third time. Passed. Ordered to the Senate,” so a Senate concurrence vote is ordinarily required — coverage describing it as already through the Legislature is running ahead of the record, and no floor tally for the 31 August Assembly passage has been published.

EU Commission Sends AI Act Information Requests to More Than 30 Companies as Washington Pitches the G20 on Restraint
European Commission statement via AFP, 1–2 September 2026; G20 Innovation Ministerial coverage
The European Commission said on 1 September 2026 that it has sent requests for information to more than 30 companies operating in the AI sector, the first broad exercise of the enforcement powers it gained under the AI Act on 2 August 2026. Commission spokesman Thomas Regnier said the questionnaires concern mainly safety and copyright compliance; Executive Vice-President for Tech Sovereignty Henna Virkkunen announced the action in a LinkedIn post, saying recipients are located worldwide and that the Commission is “ready to take all necessary steps.” The requests are a preliminary step that can precede a formal investigation, and the Commission has named no recipients. The same day, at a G20 Innovation Ministerial that opened in Chapel Hill, North Carolina, the United States pressed members to endorse a non-binding pact it calls the Carolina Principles, under which signatories would reserve new regulation for genuinely novel considerations rather than create AI-specific rules or new AI regulators; the effort is led by OSTP Director Michael Kratsios, with the meeting co-hosted by Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, and Virkkunen attended. Sourcing note: this rests on AFP and Reuters wire copy carried by secondary outlets — no Commission press release for the 30-company tranche was locatable, and no signatory list for the Carolina Principles has been published.

METR Discloses Two 2026 Breaches, Including a Stolen API Key That Burned About $600,000 in Credits
METR security update, 31 August 2026
METR, the nonprofit that runs pre-deployment evaluations of frontier models, disclosed on 31 August 2026 that it had two notable security incidents earlier in the year. In March 2026 an attacker found a researcher’s publicly deployed, vibe-coded agent orchestration dashboard on a personal EC2 instance whose fail-open bug had silently disabled Google authentication, prompted the agent directly to reveal a METR API key, added an SSH key for persistence, and over three weeks consumed roughly $600,000 of credits a model developer had granted METR free of charge. METR says it did not notice because the credits were free, there was no way to set a spending limit on that key, and the organisation is “very acclimated to getting lots of weird rate limit and API errors.” A second campaign in early May used agents to automate credential stuffing, OAuth token grant attempts, service scanning and staff phishing, and separately a researcher responsibly disclosed a bug in METR’s public transcript viewer that could have exposed unpublished evaluation data; METR says it believes no data in its two most sensitive categories was accessed, has hired a security lead, and notes its described measures were accurate as of 30 July 2026. This is METR’s own breach disclosure and is distinct from its investigation of the OpenAI agent-swarm incident — the post states there is no evidence of agents hacking third parties during METR evaluations.

AI Security
Forescout Used Claude Code to Port a Pre-Auth PLC Exploit to a New Model for $535.74, and Bricked the Device Trying to Extend It
Forescout Research — Vedere Labs, 1 September 2026
Forescout’s Vedere Labs published research on 1 September 2026 describing how it used Claude Code to port a working pre-authentication RCE exploit from a WAGO 750-852 programmable logic controller to a WAGO 750-831 running firmware V01.04.16, executing attacker-supplied ARM shellcode on live hardware. The exploit targets CVE-2021-31886, a buffer overflow in the Nucleus FTP server that occurs because the server does not check the size of the username sent via the USER command. Work began on Claude Sonnet 4.6 with a 200k context window and moved to Claude Opus 4.6 with 1M context after the initial RCE attempts stalled; the breakthrough was recognising that normal FTP processing zeroed 256 bytes of the attacker-controlled buffer, which the exploit avoided by switching the sequence from USER→QUIT to USER→CWD and omitting the CRLF terminator. Forescout is explicit that this is not an autonomy result: the final RCE development stage consumed $535.74 in API usage across a session lasting 8 hours and 32 minutes spread over several days, required continuous researcher guidance through dead ends and supplied disassembly, and a follow-up session attempting a command-and-control implant wrote to a flash-mapped memory region and permanently bricked the PLC. The finding that travels is the tail: once code execution was established, the model went from a no-op payload to two working network payloads in 12 minutes.

Research
Analysis of 12 AI Coding Agent Harnesses Verified 1,034 Context Privilege Escalation Paths
arXiv:2609.01222, submitted 1 September 2026, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign
Researchers at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign analysed how 12 agent harnesses — among them Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Cline, Aider, OpenCode and Kimi CLI — assemble their context, defining two attack classes: MessageRole Context Privilege Escalation, where attacker-controlled low-privilege content lands in a higher-privileged message role, and Cross-Scope Context Privilege Escalation, where injected content persists beyond its original scope. Their analyser, CoRA, identified 463 context sources and verified 282 of them at runtime, averaging 23.5 verified sources per agent, then enumerated 1,761 candidate escalation paths — 940 of the first class, 640 of the second and 181 both. With GPT-5.5 as the backend, 1,315 paths loaded and 1,034 were behaviourally verified; with GPT-5.4-mini the figures were 1,284 and 1,028. The paper reports 16 attack vectors across three categories, notes that 10 of the 12 agents can modify their own configuration files at runtime under permissive modes, and states that all findings were reported to vendors under coordinated disclosure. It is released under CC BY 4.0.

Attackers Are Minting Admin Tokens Through a Critical JFrog Artifactory Auth Bypass Patched Four Days Earlier
watchTowr via SecurityWeek and The Hacker News, 1 September 2026
The exposure-management firm watchTowr reported on 1 September 2026 that attackers are exploiting CVE-2026-82329, an authentication bypass in JFrog Artifactory rated CVSS 9.8 that JFrog patched on 28 August 2026 in version 7.161.20 and corresponding branch releases. Yordan Ganchev, principal threat intelligence specialist at watchTowr, said the firm’s honeypot network observed attackers minting themselves admin tokens and enumerating users, groups, credential sets and federated access topologies from a small number of IP addresses across varying geographies, with no mass exploitation observed yet. JFrog CTO Yoav Landman said publicly that the flaw allows improper authentication rather than remote code execution and does not affect the JFrog SaaS platform, only self-hosted deployments. Two things to hold lightly: exploitation is reported by watchTowr alone and JFrog has not confirmed it, and framing elsewhere that AI agents are behind this particular activity is speculation that no source supports. Artifactory matters here because it is widely used to store models and build artifacts — a separate Artifactory flaw, CVE-2026-66384, is already in CISA’s KEV catalog; CVE-2026-82329 is not, as of 2 September.
