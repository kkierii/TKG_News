Nvidia’s board reinstates Jensen Huang as chief executive, 11 days after he resigned
NVIDIA · 23 November 2026
Nvidia’s board voted unanimously on Monday to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, chief executive and director with immediate effect, saying input from senior US government officials left it confident his return “poses no national security risk.” Huang resigned on 12 November after admitting an affair with Alice Huang, a Megaspeed executive US officials suspect of spying for China; Colette Kress returns to being CFO only and Harvey Jones stays as chair. Policy & Regulation
UN summit in Bridgetown closes with a call for a global AI risk management framework
UN News · 23 November 2026
Closing the Bridgetown Special Summit on AI Safety, co-hosted by the UN and Barbados, Secretary-General António Guterres called on member states to begin talks on a multilateral AI Risk Management Framework covering shared safety measures, common testing, transparency requirements and independent evaluation of advanced models. He warned that AI is driving “the biggest transfer of power in the history of humankind,” from states to a small number of companies and people. Barbados Prime Minister Mia Mottley agreed that a frontier lab should pay if its agent hacked a Barbadian bank, and that governments should hold a “kill switch” over models they buy. In a video message, French President Emmanuel Macron said 18 heads of state and government and 11 AI leaders have signed the Paris Call for AI Security.

Research
Anthropic posts an AI-written proof that VP ≠ VNP, checked in Lean but not yet by a human
Anthropic on GitHub · 23 November 2026
Anthropic published a paper and a Lean 4 formalisation, both written by AI models, claiming that over every field, circuits for the Hamiltonian cycle polynomial, and for the permanent outside characteristic 2, need more than m^(c·(log m)^(1/3)) gates. If correct, that makes VP a proper subset of VNP, the algebraic counterpart of P versus NP, though the README says it does not settle P versus NP itself. The result was found by an internal research Claude model, and the repository states that no human mathematician has checked the proof. Lean and a second, independently written checker, nanoda, verify the proofs, but no machine checks that the statement file says what is claimed.

AI Security
CISA lists the MONAI Label flaw AI agents exploited, now CVE-2026-133968, with a 26 November deadline
SecurityOnline · 24 November 2026
CISA added CVE-2026-133968, a path traversal in Project MONAI’s MONAI Label medical-imaging annotation server, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 23 November and gave federal agencies until 26 November to fix it. A datastore upload endpoint trusted a caller-supplied image name, letting attackers write files anywhere the server can and run code; authentication is off by default and the flaw is rated CVSS 9.8. All versions up to 0.8.5 are affected, including the projectmonai/monailabel Docker image, and 0.8.6, released on 22 November, fixes it. Amazon attributed the exploitation to autonomous agents built on the Cairn framework; CISA also added an N-able MailAssure SSRF flaw, CVE-2026-134624.

AI Security
Wiz details a wave of cloud intrusions run with open-source agentic attack frameworks
Wiz Research · 23 November 2026
Wiz Research says agentic offensive frameworks such as ARTEX, which is open source with hundreds of forks, along with Cairn, CyberStrikeAI and HexStrike, are driving a surge in cloud intrusions, and that disclosed incidents have more than tripled since its earlier “Black October” post. Censys counted more than 1,400 ARTEX consoles exposed to the internet in early November, often still on default credentials. In one campaign the operator rotated through more than 220 residential and ISP proxy IPs against a single target; in another, the attacker’s exposed files showed forged admin tokens reaching 3,392 users and 50 decrypted SSH private keys. Wiz says the activity spans many operators and should not be read as one campaign.

TechCrunch · 23 November 2026
Amazon said it will acquire the technology behind the Decart Optimization Stack, software that helps AI models run efficiently on Nvidia, Amazon and Google hardware, along with the Israeli startup’s founders and its AI research and engineering team. Peter DeSantis, Amazon’s senior vice president for AI models and custom silicon, said more efficient compute means better training and lower-cost inference.

Research
DeepMind uses teams of Gemini 4 agents to cut the cost of quantum algorithms, checking each step in Lean
Google DeepMind · 23 November 2026
Google DeepMind researchers ran independent teams of Gemini 4 agents that proposed improvements to known quantum algorithms, verified each idea in Lean before adding it to a shared notebook, and compared results at virtual meetings every four hours. For a circuit used in inertial confinement fusion, they report an 11.8× cut in computing cost and a drop from 5,650 to 4,338 error-corrected qubits; for the drug-metabolising enzyme cytochrome P450, a 2.5× improvement and 481 to 333 qubits. They also report a 2.57× speed-up for an implementation of Grover’s search and 290× for the arcsin routine. The authors note the algorithms would still need more resources than early error-corrected hardware will support.
