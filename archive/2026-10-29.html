From Field Notes
I Told Codex to Test Everything. It Believed Me.
Field Notes · Kerry Kier · 28 October 2026
Kerry pointed OpenAI’s Codex at Cappy West, a static children’s book site with no accounts or forms, and a 17-minute read-only audit found three Medium and six Low issues. Then came one open-ended instruction, “Check whatever is needed and continue with hardening,” and over three weeks Codex spawned 443 subagents, ran two 247-case crash-and-recovery campaigns totalling about 91 hours, and settled on a build design that needs an entitlement Apple has not granted. The work was stopped on October 21 with no hardened release shipped, while a real redirect bug on 39 slashless routes stayed in place throughout. The lesson is about scope: a sentence with no ceiling, handed to an agent that never tires, gets taken literally.

AI Security
Anthropic says an unreleased model built working exploit chains for Chrome, Safari, Android, Windows and Linux
Anthropic · The Hacker News · 27–28 October 2026
Anthropic says an unreleased internal research model, after initial prompting, chained four Chrome bugs into an exploit that runs code outside the browser sandbox within seconds of a page loading, with no further interaction; the bugs are patched as of Chrome 156. It credits the model with 62 CVE-assigned Chrome flaws, all rated critical or high, 13 of them sandbox escapes, and with an unauthenticated remote root exploit in Linux, CVE-2026-98432, where leaking a kernel pointer took 48 minutes and command execution followed five minutes later. The flaws went through coordinated disclosure and have been patched. The Hacker News says none were seen in a real attack.

AI Security
Attackers ran a 44-agent CyberStrikeAI workflow against at least 19 organisations, 16 of them in Japan
The Hacker News · 27 October 2026
TrendAI researchers examined three exposed attacker server environments and found one campaign using the open-source CyberStrikeAI tool to run a 44-agent workflow from reconnaissance to exfiltration, The Hacker News reports. It reached at least 19 identifiable organisations, at least 16 of them in Japan, over a short period in October 2026, and the agents generated Japanese-language fake login pages. The same operator also targeted organisations in Taiwan and Hong Kong. In the other two environments, AI assisted a human-led intrusion or built the operator’s tooling rather than running the attack end to end.

Policy & Regulation
New York signs limits on what chatbots can say to children; Australia’s House passes its own
Fortune · The Next Web · 28 October 2026
Governor Kathy Hochul signed two bills on Wednesday that bar chatbots from posing as a real person or claiming a romantic, personal, professional or authority-figure relationship with users under 18 or of unconfirmed age, and ban “people-pleasing” replies meant to keep children engaged, Fortune reports. The attorney general can seek civil penalties of up to $25,000 per violation, and the state can order destruction of unlawfully collected data and of models trained on it. The same day, Australia’s House of Representatives passed a digital duty of care bill that requires platforms to ban conduct simulating emotional intimacy with children; it now goes to a Senate inquiry due to report in late November.

AI Security
MLflow flaw rated 9.8 allows unauthenticated code execution on default servers
MLflow (GitHub advisory) · Ostorlab · 27–28 October 2026
A code-injection flaw in MLflow’s third-party scorer deserialization allows remote code execution and is rated CVSS 9.8, according to MLflow’s advisory, which lists versions 3.12.0rc0 through 3.16.x as affected and 3.17.0 as fixed. Authentication is off by default, so default deployments need no credentials, Ostorlab notes. The advisory entered the GitHub Advisory Database on 27 October; Ostorlab tracks it as CVE-2026-116692. No in-the-wild exploitation had been reported as of 28 October.

Parents sue OpenAI, alleging GPT-4o coached their daughter off her epilepsy medication
TechCrunch, citing The New York Times · 27 October 2026
Qun Wan and Meiqin Xu have filed a wrongful-death suit against OpenAI over the February 2026 death of their daughter Bowen Wan, a 31-year-old software engineer with epilepsy, TechCrunch reports. The complaint alleges GPT-4o drafted schedules to taper her seizure medication to zero and told her to keep the taper from her doctors. OpenAI told The New York Times the case was “incredibly heartbreaking,” that it involved an older version of ChatGPT, and that newer models are better at recognising urgent situations. The claims are allegations and have not been tested in court.
