The US and eight partners sign a declaration to shield AI supply chains from China
Financial Times via Newsmax · 15 November 2026
The United States, Japan, Australia, Israel, the UK, South Korea, India, Qatar and the UAE have agreed to draw up a joint list of critical technologies and supply chains behind AI that need more protection from China, the Financial Times reported on Sunday. The San Francisco Declaration, part of the US-led Pax Silica initiative, covers semiconductors, critical minerals and energy infrastructure, sets up working groups on supply-chain weak points and calls for stronger protection against theft or unauthorised transfer of technology. Germany, Sweden, the Netherlands and the European Commission are expected to sign next week. It lands as President Trump prepares to meet Xi Jinping at the APEC summit in Shenzhen.

AI Security
Amazon says Chinese-nexus groups ran ARTEX agents to steal AI model keys and fake identity checks
AWS Security Blog · 13 November 2026
Amazon’s MadPot decoy sensors, posing as LLM inference APIs, captured requests from ARTEX agents between 3 October and 5 November that carried the agents’ own records of their campaigns, CISO CJ Moses wrote. The largest campaign stole AI model keys from exposed gateways and app builders including One API, LiteLLM, Dify, Langflow, RAGFlow, Open WebUI and Ollama, using flaws such as CVE-2025-3248 (Langflow), CVE-2026-28797 (RAGFlow) and LiteLLM’s default master key, and fed stolen keys back in to pay for more hunting. A second campaign tried to pass identity checks at two cryptocurrency exchanges with real-time deepfakes; the vendor’s server rejected every recorded attempt. Amazon attributes the activity, with moderate confidence, to separate Chinese-nexus groups.

Incidents
Uber says hackers reached driver data through its account with Fireworks AI
Gizmodo · 13 November 2026
Uber’s breach notices say a third-party provider told it on 9 October that its internal credentials had been stolen, and that on 13 October Uber found attackers had used them to reach Uber data, including drivers’ personal information. Uber confirmed to Gizmodo that the provider was Fireworks AI, the inference company whose breach also exposed Notion customer data. State filings put at least 7,870 people affected in Rhode Island (1,873), South Carolina (3,430) and Massachusetts (2,567), with no national total given. Uber says it cut its data connections with Fireworks and is offering free credit monitoring.

Chips & Infrastructure
Nvidia disputed a manager’s chip-smuggling charges with Taiwanese officials, Bloomberg reports
The Next Web · 15 November 2026
Nvidia representatives told Taiwanese political officials they disagreed with the charges against a manager surnamed Chang and that he should not be under investigation, Bloomberg reported, citing people familiar with the matter; the officials said they had no role in the case. Keelung prosecutors call Chang, indicted in August for forgery and breach of trust, the core figure in a network that sent 74 Nvidia-equipped servers to China, with another 56 seized. Nvidia said its investigation found no sign the employee knew of the alleged diversion, and TSMC declined Nvidia’s request for advice. Bloomberg calls it the first publicly known prosecution of an Nvidia employee over chip diversion.

Nvidia’s $500bn AI financing platform faces a slower path without Huang, analysts tell Reuters
Reuters via AOL · 16 November 2026
Nvidia’s financing partners, including Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR, are still discussing deals, two people told Reuters, but analysts expect new deals to take longer or cost more after Jensen Huang stepped down as chief executive on Thursday. The platform, unveiled in August with Nvidia’s guarantees capped at 25%, has yet to announce a deal. Bank of America estimates Nvidia had made $70bn of equity investments in AI companies by August and provided $230bn in financial guarantees, including up to $105bn for OpenAI’s Ohio data-centre lease. Morgan Stanley expects the money to tilt toward established hyperscalers.

Research
A physicist and GPT-6 Astra propose a candidate lattice definition of the Standard Model
Blockchain.News · 15 November 2026
Nathaniel Craig, who leads UC Santa Barbara’s particle theory group and is a visiting scientist at OpenAI, has proposed with GPT-6 Astra a candidate nonperturbative definition of the Standard Model on a spacetime lattice, set out in an October 2026 preprint. Craig had a strategy but could not prove it worked in every case; Astra turned it into a concrete construction and developed the detailed proof. The central obstacle is the chirality of the weak force, which standard lattice formulations struggle to represent. OpenAI’s own post blocked our fetch, so this rests on Blockchain.News’s account.

Apple and OpenEvidence launch a study app on how people use AI for health
TechCrunch · 15 November 2026
Apple has launched a study app with OpenEvidence that answers health questions using clinical sources including the New England Journal of Medicine, JAMA and Nature. It is open to US users over 21 on an iPhone 15 or later running iOS 26 or later with Apple Intelligence on, and aims for up to 200,000 participants over more than 12 months. Apple says data is anonymised and will not be used to train AI models, and that OpenEvidence deletes queries and responses after 24 hours.
