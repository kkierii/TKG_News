Anthropic prices its IPO at $83 a share, raising $81.3 billion ahead of today’s Nasdaq debut
CNBC · 18 November 2026
Anthropic priced its initial public offering at $83 a share on Wednesday, above its $70 to $78 range, and upsized the deal to about 980 million shares, raising $81.3 billion, more than the $75 billion SpaceX raised initially in June. The stock is expected to begin trading on Nasdaq today under the ticker “ANT”, at a fully diluted market value of around $1.5 trillion. Its prospectus shows revenue of $33.24 billion for the nine months to 30 September, up roughly 1,200%, and a $104 billion net loss, mostly from fair-value changes on convertible notes and derivatives. Google, Microsoft, Nvidia and AMD are among companies that agreed to invest a combined $26 billion at the IPO price.

Google releases Gemini 4 Neon at $3 input and $15 output per million tokens
Google · 18 November 2026
Gemini 4 Neon is rolling out in the Gemini API and Google AI Studio, Gemini for business, Google Antigravity, and the Gemini app for AI Pro and Ultra subscribers. It costs $3 per million input tokens ($0.15 cached) and $15 per million output tokens, with a 1 million-token context window. Google says Neon scores 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its xhigh setting, third overall, with a 32% lower cost per task than Gemini 4 Carbon; the benchmark and cost figures are Google’s own. A cyber-defence version is rolling out to members of Google’s Fairwind Program over the coming days.

Oregon sues OpenAI for $2.035 billion over ChatGPT’s safety for children
Oregon Department of Justice · 17 November 2026
Attorney General Dan Rayfield filed the complaint in Multnomah County Circuit Court as part of a new “Keeping Kids Safe” initiative. The state alleges OpenAI broke Oregon’s Unlawful Trade Practices Act by misleading the public about how safe ChatGPT is for children, selling a product with undisclosed safety problems and marketing it unfairly to minors, and also claims negligence, unjust profit and public nuisance. Oregon is asking the court to order OpenAI to stop the alleged misstatements and put working protections in place. The claims are allegations; the release carries no response from OpenAI.

AI Security
Researchers take over the Hugging Face account behind the Kokoro text-to-speech model
BleepingComputer · 17 November 2026
Kokoro has nearly 13 million monthly downloads on Hugging Face and is supported in tools from Google, NVIDIA and Intel, though its developer has not pushed an update since April 2025. Researchers led by Alon Hertz registered the lapsed hexgrad.com domain listed in the project’s DONATE.md file and used it to reset the publisher account’s password. They then altered the model’s weights in 87 seconds on a gaming GPU so it spoke text different from its input, testing only locally and uploading nothing. Hertz says such an update would look like a legitimate release and contain no code for malware scanners to catch.

Policy & Regulation
Senator Ricketts introduces the MEGA Act to force a decision on blacklisting Megaspeed
Office of Sen. Pete Ricketts · 17 November 2026
The bill would give the Commerce Department 30 days to decide whether to add Megaspeed, a Singapore-based buyer of advanced Nvidia chips, to the Entity List, with 30-day extensions if the Attorney General certifies that the deadline would jeopardise a federal criminal investigation. It would immediately require export licences for transactions involving Megaspeed, with a presumption of denial, and allow restrictions on related companies. The release says Commerce’s 16 November chip rules do not resolve Megaspeed’s status, and that State, War, Energy and Treasury have supported the listing, citing press reports.

AI Security
Canonical offers Ubuntu Pro customers AI-written security patches before human review
Help Net Security · 18 November 2026
The patches come from Surge, a Canonical system that fixes known vulnerabilities in Ubuntu’s userspace packages, and ship through a new opt-in channel called the Edge archive. Several competing models each write a backport, a panel of judge models must reach consensus, and deadlocked or low-confidence cases go to a human on the security team. Kernel fixes are excluded, and Ubuntu’s regular security updates still get human validation. Canonical says its security teams have shipped twice as many fixes as last year.

Policy & Regulation
Senators Cantwell and Rounds urge the White House to speed up AI cyber defence
The Record · 18 November 2026
In a letter on Tuesday to Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton, who chairs the White House AI task force, Sens. Maria Cantwell (D-Wash.) and Mike Rounds (R-S.D.) warned that AI is “rapidly increasing the ability to discover and exploit weaknesses in software”. They asked the task force to speed up defensive AI tools, set security standards for autonomous AI agents and convene AI developers, security firms, critical infrastructure operators, NIST and CISA. The letter cites AI-assisted intrusions in Japan and banks’ concerns that they cannot tell rogue AI agents from ones acting for customers.

Research
RoboBuilder: coding agents design robots, and most simulated passes fail on a real course at first
arXiv · 18 November 2026
The benchmark has coding agents co-design a robot’s hardware and control program, and every design that passes in simulation is built and tested physically. Across four frontier coding agents, the authors ran 16,427 simulations of 12,774 robots and built 135 physical robots in an estimated 240 hours. Using public part data, none of the first 15 designs that passed in simulation crossed the real course, and 66% of simulated passes held up in the real world across all levels. Showing an agent video of its real failure removed 72% of level-1 and level-2 failures.
