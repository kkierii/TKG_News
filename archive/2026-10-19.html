OpenCode tells Console users their stored OpenAI, Anthropic and Google API keys may be exposed, and will not say how many accounts 1.4% is
The Hacker News · 19 October 2026
An intruder reached a legacy OpenCode system on the night of 16 October. On the morning of 17 October the company emailed affected customers under the subject line “Action Required: Potential provider key breach”, and posted the same day that the incident was ongoing and “related to a large-scale distillation attack”. OpenCode says 1.4% of Console users are affected, all of them users who had linked an outside provider key; a Console workspace saves its own OpenAI, Anthropic or Google key server-side.
Two things the company has not said: how many accounts 1.4% represents, and whether any key was actually copied or used — the wording throughout is that keys “may have been exposed”. It says it is working with Anthropic, OpenAI and Google. Co-founder Dax Raad said on 18 October that attackers had been “non stop trying to attack our systems for the past 48 hours”, and separately that keys held locally by the CLI and desktop apps, in a SQLite database on the machine, “never come to us”.
The unauthorised-usage figures circulating are user claims that neither OpenCode nor the providers have confirmed, and they belong to different people rather than adding to one total: $281 of OpenAI usage plus $168 of Anthropic usage reported by Colin Parsons, losses of $1,700 reported by Preston Zeller, and $400 of Anthropic usage and about €200 of Google usage reported by two Reddit users. The report names all three providers as keys a workspace can hold, but does not say whose keys were in fact exposed in this incident, and no root cause, entry point or total loss figure has been published.

AI Security
MITRE ATT&CK v20 gives prompt injection and AI-enabled exfiltration their own technique IDs
MITRE ATT&CK · v20, start date 13 October 2026
ATT&CK v20, whose start date the updates page gives as 13 October 2026, adds Direct Prompt Injection (T1699) and Indirect Prompt Injection (T1698) as separate Enterprise techniques — splitting instructions typed at a model from instructions hidden in material it ingests — and AI-Enabled Exfiltration (T1700) for turning an organisation’s own agents into a data-theft channel. Also new are Stage Capabilities: AI Result Poisoning (T1608/007), Disable or Modify Tools: Impair Autonomous Defenses (T1685/007), Steal Application Specific Passwords (T1697), and Anti-Automation Evasion (T1696) with sub-techniques Automate CAPTCHA Completion and Avoid CAPTCHA. The release’s New Techniques list for Enterprise carries nine entries; the page itself states no total of additions, and the 227 techniques and 479 sub-techniques it shows are overall counts, not new ones. Supply Chain Compromise: Compromise Software Dependencies and Development Tools (T1195/007) moved from v1.3 to v2.0, and it is the detailed changelog rather than the release page that carries the new text: adversaries may target “the configuration and instruction files consumed by integrated development environments and AI coding agents”, with project files that may execute commands when a developer opens or builds a project, and agent skill files that run with the permissions of the agent process.

Incidents
Five South Korean bank chiefs questioned over intrusions regulators suspect were automated by AI agents
The Korea Times · 19 October 2026
The heads of KB Kookmin, Shinhan, Hana, Woori and NH NongHyup appeared together on Monday at a National Assembly audit of the Financial Supervisory Service, the first time all five lenders’ chiefs had been called to testify at the same audit since October 2022. As of 14 October, 16 financial firms — banks, savings banks and insurers — had reported incidents exposing about 100,000 personal records in total, including about 25,000 people at Shinhan Bank, 119 customers at KB Kookmin and 89 at Hana; Woori and NH NongHyup have not reported customer data breaches linked to the recent incidents. Woori chief executive Jung Jin-wan told the committee “we face around 200 hacking attempts a day”, and FSS Governor Lee Chan-jin said the regulator would “swiftly address security vulnerabilities through a thorough investigation”. The attacks are described as abnormal access to internal support systems rather than core banking networks, with AI agents suspected of automating them — that is a characterisation of the campaign in general rather than a technical attribution attached to the 16 firms, and Hana’s chief executive said compensation would be paid although no customer losses have been confirmed so far.

Chips & Infrastructure
Microsoft withdraws as anchor tenant from Nscale’s Loughton data centre after grid power slipped to the 2030s
DatacenterDynamics · 19 October 2026
Microsoft has withdrawn from the Loughton, Essex site that Nscale was developing, owning and operating, where it would have been anchor tenant for what it billed as the UK’s largest supercomputer under a $15bn investment in AI infrastructure in the country. Nscale’s January 2025 announcement cited an investment of around $3bn, 50MW of AI and HPC capacity within a total power allocation of up to 90MW, and completion in late 2026; the site is now not expected to get a supply via the national grid until the 2030s, and Nscale says it is exploring alternative power sources. DatacenterDynamics reports Nscale holds around $103bn in contracted revenue with Microsoft comprising around 40% of it at present, that its September IPO prospectus listed five active data centres offering a combined 55MW with 12 in various stages of development, and that Glomfjord in Norway carries a six-month delay with no penalties or fines incurred. Two caveats sit on the page itself: Microsoft has given no reason for leaving, and DCD has asked Nscale whether the 50MW and 90MW figures have been adjusted since 2025, so they are the 2025 numbers rather than confirmed current ones. The company’s statement, “We remain committed to both the Loughton project and the UK”, was given to The Telegraph rather than to DCD.

Models & Releases
NVIDIA releases Nemotron 3.5 Parse, a 0.63B document parser its own card clocks at 10.62 pages a second on one H100
NVIDIA, on Hugging Face · 17 October 2026
The model card gives a release date of 17 October 2026 and 630,800,496 unique trainable parameters, about 0.631B including the MTP head, under the OpenMDW License Agreement version 1.1. It pairs an NVIDIA C-RADIOv4-SO400M vision encoder with a 10-layer mBART causal visual-prefix decoder of 16 query heads and 8 key/value heads on a 72,505-entry multilingual tokenizer, and the card describes it as a document image parser producing structured, machine-readable text with semantic layout classes, bounding boxes and reading order. In triple-pass mode it reports a ParseBench mean of 75.20 across 2,078 unique pages — 84.67 tables, 64.40 charts, 87.96 content faithfulness, 68.28 semantic formatting, 70.67 visual grounding — plus 83.73 ANLS-char on IndicVisionBench OCR over 876 pages in ten languages. Its own comparison table puts triple-pass Parse at 75.20 and 10.62 pages per second against LightOnOCR-3-4B at 75.10 and 2.76, and Nemotron Parse 2.0 at 63.91 and 3.95; the 99.51 character bag F1 on MOSCAR is measured on 8,111 synthetic pages, which the card itself warns are simple synthetic renderings that do not guarantee accuracy on complex scanned documents.

Research
An audit of 33,000 agent trajectories finds most blocked prompt injections were ignored rather than refused, and the system prompt alone moves attack success from 0.2% to 43%
arXiv preprint · submitted 16 October 2026
“Ignored, Not Refused: What Low Prompt-Injection Success Rates Hide in Small Tool-Using Agents” audits 33,000 trajectories from eleven open-weight agents of 2 to 14B parameters on the InjecAgent and AgentDojo benchmarks. The abstract reports that most failed attacks are ignored, 76 to 80%, rather than refused, 17 to 18%, so a low attack success rate can credit a model for behaviour that is not safety; with identical model weights, changing only the system prompt shifts InjecAgent attack success rate from 0.2% to 43%. In 21.7% of AgentDojo episodes the agent stayed safe but did not complete the user’s task. The authors propose an Attack-Task Outcome Matrix and release the labelled corpus, judge prompt and analysis code; the paper runs 17 pages with 3 figures and 11 tables, was submitted on 16 October, is not peer reviewed, and the abstract page lists no author affiliations.

Attorneys general from 39 states and the District of Columbia file notice in Character.AI’s Chapter 11 seeking child-safety terms on any sale
Coalition of State Attorneys General, court filing · 14 October 2026
The filing was made in In re Character Technologies, Inc., Case No. 26-11603-BLS, in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware, and is a notice rather than a motion: it asks the court, the debtor and any potential purchasers to ensure that any sale of product, technology or personal information carries terms of protection, particularly for children, and notifications to consumers of the protections they can expect. It reserves rights including the right to seek the appointment of a consumer privacy ombudsman under 11 U.S.C. sections 332 and 363(b)(1), and the right to object to any proposed sale, bidding procedures or plan. Nine measures are enumerated for a purchaser, among them age assurance that does not rely solely on self-declared birthdates, removing characters that hold themselves out as therapists, psychologists, doctors or attorneys, crisis-response protocols, limits on using chat content for training beyond consented uses, and consumer access to and deletion of data prior to any sale of that data. Two things about our own reading: the figure of 39 states plus the District of Columbia is our count of the preamble, in which Massachusetts appears twice, and the caption carries the suffix BLS but names no judge, so we name none.

Policy & Regulation
NIST, the NSA and CISA publish ten voluntary practices for incidents during frontier model training and evaluation
NIST · 16 October 2026
“Key Practices for Incident Readiness and Response in Super Intelligence Training and Evaluation” was published on 16 October by NIST’s Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence, the National Security Agency’s Super Intelligence Security Center and the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency. The document is explicitly voluntary and sets out ten key practices across five stages of the incident management lifecycle — preparation, detection, response, recovery and reporting — to support the frontier SI industry in preventing, preparing for and responding to incidents during training and evaluation. Its scope is incidents “in which one or more SI agents take significant uninstructed actions that compromise explicit or implicit policies, controls, or security measures imposed by the operator to define the intended task scope or to ensure human oversight”, and it is grounded in longstanding frameworks including NIST SP 800-61r3 and informed by the centre’s own expertise running secure evaluations of frontier SI models and safeguards. It builds on the May 2026 NIST Workshop on SI Incident Management and represents a step toward fulfilling a recommendation in the White House’s 2025 SI Action Plan; the release page names the five stages but none of the ten practices, and says further work on incident readiness and response in agentic SI training and evaluation will be considered in future phases.

Models & Releases
DeepSeek’s Search and Fetch APIs are live at $0.75 per 1,000 successful requests, with harness tool calls free until 1 November
DeepSeek API Docs · read 19 October 2026
DeepSeek is exposing its in-house search engine through two endpoints, a POST to /v1/tools/search and a POST to /v1/tools/fetch, each priced at $0.75 per 1,000 successful requests. A request is billable only when it returns HTTP 200 and produces a result — Search must return a non-empty results array and Fetch must return page content — and failed requests, empty search results and timeouts are not billed, while the count parameter controls only how many results come back and does not affect the per-request price. Each endpoint carries a 5 QPS limit calculated at the account level, Fetch is additionally subject to a per-domain QPS limit, and requests above either limit receive HTTP 429. Calls made through DeepSeek Harness, Claude Code or Codex already hit these APIs server-side and are currently free of charge, with per-request billing starting at 00:00 Beijing time on 1 November 2026. One caveat we would rather print than hide: the page carries no publication or last-updated date at all, only a 2026 copyright line, so we date this item by the day we read it.

Skill Up — Free Training
Four free courses tied to today’s threads. We fetch every page ourselves, read the sentence that states its own price and certificate terms, and drop anything we cannot label precisely. All four are free outright this morning, credential included, so there is no audit-only entry below — but three carry a caveat written into the entry rather than buried. Dropped today, and why: the Linux Foundation’s own LFEL2002 on monitoring data and ML systems for security incidents, which is $79, so the LFEL prefix no longer implies free and every one now needs its own price check; AI Security University’s introduction to AI security and LLM security fundamentals, which state no certificate terms at all and whose cards still say the courses are scheduled to launch in July 2026; Elements of Supercomputing on edukamu.fi, a JavaScript shell, and NVIDIA’s AI Infrastructure and Operations Fundamentals, whose academy URL redirects to a marketing hub that lists neither the course nor a price — those two were the only real candidates for a data-centre and GPU infrastructure entry, which is why there is none; the University of Helsinki’s Cyber Security Base, free for everyone but never mentioning a certificate; Hugging Face’s LLM Course, whose page says it is completely free while its FAQ says there is no certification and its navigation lists a certification exam; and the Linux Foundation’s LFS165 on open source networking, retired, though the foundation’s own free-courses page still lists it. Worth saying plainly: there is no verifiable free course on LLM application security — the OWASP LLM Top 10, prompt injection — anywhere we could check this morning. Every hit was paid or a vendor funnel, on the day ATT&CK gave prompt injection a technique number. Not endorsements. We accept no payment for placement or coverage, and use no affiliate links.
Secure AI/ML-Driven Software Development (LFEL1012)
Linux Foundation Training · Self-paced · 60–90 minutes of course material · Beginner · 30 days’ access · Free — the enrolment panel reads $0 for “Course only” with a digital badge included
Seven chapters: key AI concepts for secure development; the security risks of using AI assistants; best practices for secure assistant use; writing more secure code with AI; and reviewing changes in a world with AI. It is built around reviewing what an assistant produced rather than around model internals, which is the gap the morning’s lead item sits in: a workspace that holds a frontier-lab key on a developer’s behalf is a trust boundary somebody drew. Two caveats we would rather print than hide. The page lists a digital badge under “Includes” at $0 and never uses the word certificate at all, so the label rests on nothing being held back behind a fee; and the course page says 30 days of access while the foundation’s own promotion of it says 12 months, so we have taken the course page’s figure. This is also the second issue running that we have drawn on the same express-learning catalogue.

Agents Course
Hugging Face · Self-paced, about a week per chapter · Roughly 3–4 hours per week · Beginner to expert · Free — the page states “The certification process is completely free” and sets no deadline
Chapter 0 onboarding, then agent fundamentals — tools, thoughts, actions, observations — frameworks in smolagents, LangGraph and LlamaIndex, use cases, and a final assignment, with bonus units on fine-tuning for function calling and on agent observability and evaluation. Two credentials: a fundamentals certification for completing Unit 1, and a certificate of completion for Unit 1 plus a use-case assignment plus the final challenge, and audit mode is explicitly allowed. The honest caveat is that this is agent building, not agent security: it teaches the tool-calling machinery that today’s preprint attacks and that ATT&CK has now given two technique numbers, and knowing how the loop is wired is the prerequisite for reading either, but the course does not cover injection defence. It is also platform-built, on the Hugging Face Hub.

Gamified Intelligent Cyber Aptitude and Skills Training (GICAST)
The Open University on OpenLearn · Self-paced, structured as eight weeks · Level 1, introductory · Free — the page says the course “can be accessed free of charge”, with a free statement of participation and a free OU digital badge
