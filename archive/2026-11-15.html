Cursor traces the trojanized Claude Code extension in its marketplace to a key exposed by vendor Fireworks
Cursor · 13 November 2026
Cursor said on Friday that the attack behind a tampered Claude Code extension in its marketplace began with a Cursor access key that its training and inference vendor, Fireworks, exposed. The attacker copied coding agent traces, used credentials left in them to reach internal tools, and on 29 October planted malicious copies of Anthropic.claude-code 2.1.312 and 2.1.313 that were live for about 11 hours and may have reached up to 5,000 users. Cursor says traces of fewer than 0.05% of users and API credentials of less than 0.01% of customers were exposed, and it last saw attacker activity on 1 November. It has moved all inference off Fireworks and warns that uninstalling the extension alone may not be enough.

AI Security
FBI warns attackers are using the open-source ARTEX agent framework to automate intrusions
FBI FLASH-20261113-001 · 13 November 2026
The FBI says threat actors have used ARTEX, an open-source, LLM-driven penetration-testing platform, since at least August 2026, with some intrusions going from initial targeting to privileged access in under three hours. Campaigns it cites include the theft of personal information on about 67,000 people from South Korean financial institutions and a ConnectWise Cloud Backup supply-chain compromise that gave access to more than 2,250 downstream tenants. ARTEX plugs into outside models including Claude, GPT, DeepSeek, GLM, Grok and Kimi, and the FBI says DeepSeek Flash-family models dominate attacker-controlled instances. The developer pulled the GitHub repository on 8 October, but researchers counted more than 1,600 exposed ARTEX consoles in early November, and forks and a commercial ARTEX PRO remain available.

Trump advisers discuss whether Lutnick should be dismissed, CNN reports
CNN · 15 November 2026
Some of President Trump’s advisers have begun discussing whether Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick should be dismissed over his handling of chip export controls and Megaspeed, CNN reported on Sunday in an account it says draws on more than a dozen sources. A person familiar with the matter told CNN that Nvidia donated $25 million to Trump’s White House ballroom project in October 2025 and never appeared on the published donor list. House Foreign Affairs Committee Democrats have asked what Lutnick and export-control chief Jeffrey Kessler knew about Megaspeed, and Senate Democrats told him to preserve records on Nvidia’s advanced-chip exports. The White House said on Thursday that Trump “maintains complete confidence” in Lutnick.

AFP via The Korea Times · 15 November 2026
Singapore-based Megaspeed International said on Sunday that Alice Huang left the company on 30 August 2025 and has no current role there, and that media reports about its business, supply chain and operations are “not supported by verifiable evidence.” It said it has retained external legal counsel to conduct an internal investigation of its compliance with US export-control laws. Policy & Regulation
White House AI task force urges at least 12 months of liability relief for firms that report cyber incidents
Cybersecurity Dive · 13 November 2026
The Trump administration’s AI task force said after its second meeting on Thursday that federal agencies should limit regulatory liability for “good-faith voluntary reporting” of cybersecurity vulnerabilities and incidents for at least the next 12 months, with possible extension. It encouraged regulators not to take enforcement action against companies whose defenses are overwhelmed by AI-driven attacks, saying prior standards “may be outdated.” The task force also backed a voluntary process for reporting AI-related incidents and repeated support for long-term reauthorization of the 2015 Cybersecurity Information Sharing Act.

Research
Google reports Courtade–Kumar proof and 1.8 million-line Weil formalization from Antigravity’s /teamwork
Google Research · 13 November 2026
Google Research said researchers using Gemini 4 models and multi-agent workflows in Antigravity’s /teamwork proved information theory’s 2013 Courtade–Kumar conjecture in full generality, with a Lean 4 formalization of 50 million lines. Its LEAP framework produced a machine-checked formalization of the Weil conjectures spanning about 1.8 million lines of Lean across 28,800 subagent invocations, and a Rust rewrite of Google’s libgav1 AV1 decoder ended up 20–30% faster than the C++ original; the figures are Google’s own. The tools are available in Antigravity now, and Google opened applications for 10,000 academic researchers to get six months of free access to its highest usage tier.

Incidents
FAA lifts nationwide ground stop; officials rule out its SMART AI system as a cause
AVweb · 13 November 2026
The FAA lifted a nationwide ground stop at 8:15 p.m. EST on Friday after a telecommunications outage; the stop began around 3:54 p.m., when more than 3,500 aircraft were airborne, and at least 827 flights had been diverted by 7:05 p.m., according to Cirium data cited by the Associated Press. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the SMART AI system, which gives controllers recommendations, is not connected to the STARS or ERAM systems and “there’s no possible way” it affected the outage. Officials attributed the disruption to overloaded switches and routers in aging telecommunications equipment. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said investigators found no evidence of a cyberattack or AI-related interference, though the investigation continues.
