Breaking
A trojanised LiteLLM 1.107.0 was live on PyPI and Docker Hub for hours, built with stolen GitHub tokens
LiteLLM security incident report · 21 October 2026
LiteLLM says malicious 1.107.0 wheels were live on PyPI from 11:39:51 to 13:23 UTC on 21 October, and that four Docker Hub tags, including latest, pointed at a compromised image until 14:20 UTC. The artifacts were built from an attacker-controlled repository using stolen GitHub CLI OAuth tokens acting through three engineers’ accounts, and they drop cook.pth and sitecustomize.py so the payload runs every time Python starts. Wiz says the stealer harvests across 16 modules, including AI service keys, and that stolen AWS credentials were in use about 4.5 hours after exfiltration. LiteLLM’s advice is to pin to 1.106.2 and rotate every key the affected hosts could reach.

AI Security
Microsoft ties the ConnectWise Cloud Backup compromise to Storm-0940 and an open-source agentic pentest framework
Microsoft Security Blog · 21 October 2026
Microsoft attributes the ConnectWise Cloud Backup supply-chain compromise to Storm-0940, which it describes as a China-state aligned actor active since 2021, using ARTEX, an open-source multi-agent LLM penetration-testing framework. A password spray from 10 to 14 October hit 327 accounts at eight organisations with more than 151,000 failed logons, peaking at 2,900 a minute on 11 October, and Microsoft observed no successful authentication from it. The actor then used ConnectWise application credentials to attempt sign-ins to more than 2,250 tenants and modified application credentials to establish persistence. Microsoft says the activity did not result from a compromise of, or a vulnerability in, its own systems, products or services.

AI Security
Wiz discloses two PHP use-after-free flaws reaching CVSS 9.2, found with AI models and patched on 21 October
Wiz · 21 October 2026
Wiz disclosed CVE-2026-109618, an upload-progress unserializer use-after-free, and CVE-2026-109619, a fatal-error backtrace use-after-free, with severity reaching CVSS 9.2 and affected versions going back to PHP 5.4. It says it found and validated them with its Atlas tool using Gemini 4 Argon and GPT-5.6 Sol, and demonstrated pre-authentication RCE on WordPress, Drupal and Joomla with the first flaw. OpenAI says Calif identified one of the flaws using GPT-5.6 Sol, that Wiz independently found both, and that it has committed $1 billion in subsidised Daybreak access for defenders. Neither page lists fixed version numbers; PHP released the patch on 21 October.

Research
Microsoft Research says human-directed AI agents helped prove the 31-year-old KLS conjecture
Microsoft Research Blog · 21 October 2026
Xinzhi Zhang of Microsoft Research and co-author Zhao Song have proved the 1995 Kannan–Lovász–Simonovits conjecture in the preprint “An O(1) Bound for the KLS Constant” (arXiv 2610.01447). AI agents explored more than 100 candidate approaches while the researchers eliminated unpromising directions, assembled the final argument and verified it; repeated unguided attempts with several leading models on a similar token budget fell short. Two further independent proofs, arXiv 2610.05474 and 2610.07728, have since appeared. The authors say they have verified a streamlined argument in Lean and will release it after final review, and none of the papers is yet peer-reviewed.

Incidents
Exposed servers show ransomware affiliates ran 133 OpenCode agent sessions through OpenRouter
SOCRadar · 20 October 2026
SOCRadar found seven exposed servers tied to affiliates of the INC Ransomware and The Gentlemen operations, holding 8,257 messages across 133 OpenCode sessions routed through OpenRouter, using models including anthropic/claude-opus-4.6 and moonshotai/kimi-k3. The records include 46 INC encryptor builds prepared for six victims and name 62 organisations, 36 of them in the United States. SOCRadar puts the operators’ recorded AI spend above $23,000; its key takeaways give $23,920. Exploitation claims in the logs, including FortiClient EMS CVE-2026-35616, are the operators’ own and are reported rather than independently confirmed.

Models & Releases
Anthropic opens a waitlisted research preview of Claude Duet, a background agent app built on Claude Opus 5.5
Anthropic · 21 October 2026
Claude Duet is a standalone app on iOS, Android and claude.ai that checks connected accounts every few hours and works in the background on a virtual machine with its own browser. Access is by waitlist, starting with US Pro and Max subscribers, and it can draw on more than 6,000 connectors and plugins. It waits for the user’s approval before sending messages, making payments, signing or deleting, and trading securities or moving money between accounts are off-limits even with approval. Anthropic describes it as an early version that may misjudge urgency.

The National · 22 October 2026
Saudi Arabia’s Humain, owned by the Public Investment Fund, widened its Nvidia partnership at GTC Berlin to up to 84 megawatts and about 27,000 GPUs. Phase one is 14MW of Blackwell HGX B300 systems, equivalent to 6,000 GPUs, deploying from the fourth quarter; phase two is 70MW of Vera Rubin, about 21,000 GPUs, planned from mid-2027, with 50MW for an unnamed US client and 20MW for Humain Cloud. Models & Releases
Recursive releases Flint, an Apache 2.0 coding model with about 2.5B active parameters aimed at laptops
Recursive model card on Hugging Face · released 21 October 2026
Flint is Recursive’s first model, a mixture-of-experts with 39.8 billion total parameters and about 2.5 billion active per token, released under Apache 2.0. The model card gives a 262,144-token context, while the launch blog says up to 512k with 256k recommended, and Recursive says it runs at more than 125 tokens a second on an M4 Pro MacBook Pro at short contexts. Its self-reported scores are 74.2% on SWE-bench Verified and 54.9% on Terminal-Bench 2.1, with no independent reproduction yet. Recursive’s blog carries no date; press coverage dates the release to 21 October.

Hugging Face · Online, self-paced, about 3–4 hours a week · Assumes basic LLM concepts and Python or TypeScript · Free — the course calls itself free and its certification page states “The certification process is completely free”
MCP fundamentals and architecture, then building and deploying an end-to-end MCP application: the plumbing that lets agents such as Claude Duet reach thousands of connectors, and the trust boundary every one of those connectors adds. 18.S997 High-Dimensional Statistics
MIT OpenCourseWare · Graduate level, lecture notes and problem sets · Free — openly licensed under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and downloadable; no certificate is offered
Opens with sub-Gaussian random variables and concentration before moving to regression, matrix estimation and minimax lower bounds, which is the probabilistic toolkit behind the KLS result. It does not cover convex geometry or KLS itself, and it assumes real analysis, linear algebra and probability, so it is the serious entry point rather than a gentle one.
