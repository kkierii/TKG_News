GPUThor Rowhammer Attack Defeats ECC on Four NVIDIA Ampere Workstation GPUs
The Hacker News, reporting University of Toronto research · 27 August 2026
University of Toronto researchers disclosed GPUThor, a non-uniform Rowhammer technique that induces bit flips on NVIDIA GDDR6 workstation GPUs and defeats the SECDED error correction that NVIDIA recommends as the mitigation for GPU Rowhammer. Four cards were tested and found vulnerable — the RTX A6000 at 48GB, RTX A5000 at 24GB, RTX A4500 at 20GB and RTX A4000 at 16GB — producing between 72,000 and 377,000 bit flips per gigabyte with ECC disabled, the A5000 highest at 377,552 per gigabyte; at 16-byte granularity the campaigns produced 387 double-bit and two triple-bit flips. With ECC enabled on an RTX A6000, one bank of hammering produced 11 detectable-uncorrectable errors and one silent data corruption over 24 hours, and the researchers reached a root shell on the host using the triple-bit corruption with the IOMMU enabled, and host-side privilege escalation via a double-bit error where the IOMMU is disabled. The attack requires only the ability to launch an unprivileged CUDA kernel on the target GPU, carries no CVE identifier and has no patch; it was reported to NVIDIA on April 29, 2026 under an embargo that lifted August 25, and no bit flips were observed on the A10, L4, L40, RTX 4090 or the HBM2e-based A30. Exploit code is scheduled for public release on November 15, 2026 at ACM CCS.

Attackers Chain Two PaperCut Flaws for Unauthenticated Code Execution
The Hacker News, reporting watchTowr and Huntress findings
PaperCut published an August 27, 2026 security bulletin covering two vulnerabilities in PaperCut NG and PaperCut MF: CVE-2026-82078, carrying a CVSS score of 9.4, an unsafe dynamic class loading condition in the database connection utilities that instantiates driver classes from configurable names without an allowlist; and CVE-2026-81578 at CVSS 8.8, an access control failure in the web management interface where unauthenticated remote requests reach administrative functions before validation completes. watchTowr reports attackers chaining the two — CVE-2026-81578 to bypass authentication, then a configuration file edit to trigger CVE-2026-82078 for remote code execution — and PaperCut states the issue affects all versions of NG and MF, with emergency patches for v25 and v26 followed by a second hardening patch. Huntress observed exploitation in two customer environments, with attackers running Base64-encoded whoami & ver commands and dropping an OS-agnostic Java class file that writes a directory listing to Udydn.out before deleting that file, server.log and derby.log. watchTowr also reports further patch bypasses affecting the latest fully patched version; one caveat on sourcing, the vendor bulletin page returned navigation chrome only when fetched, so the version and CVSS figures above trace to this reporting rather than to the vendor page directly.

Australian Police Charge Two Over the TeamPCP Compromises of Trivy, KICS and LiteLLM
The Hacker News; Help Net Security
The Australian Federal Police, working with the FBI and Western Australia Police Force, charged two Western Australian men aged 21 and 23 with a combined 14 offences over their alleged role in TeamPCP, the group behind the March 2026 compromise of the open-source scanners Trivy and Checkmarx KICS and the AI gateway LiteLLM; both were arrested August 26 and appeared in Perth Magistrates Court on August 27, 2026. The AFP said the malicious code potentially compromised more than 1,000 organisations globally, enabled theft of more than 500,000 credentials and led to exfiltration of at least 300 gigabytes of data, with global remediation costs estimated in the hundreds of millions of dollars. The compromises cascaded: credentials stolen via Trivy were used against Checkmarx KICS days later, and because LiteLLM’s build pipeline installed Trivy without pinning it to a verified version, the poisoned scanner captured LiteLLM’s publishing token, which was used to push backdoored LiteLLM releases in late March. A July 2 FBI advisory advises rotating all CI/CD secrets, publishing tokens and cloud credentials, searching for repositories named tpcp-docs and docs-tpcp, and pinning GitHub Actions workflows to commit SHA hashes.

Anthropic Reported to Sign a $45 Billion, 460MW Compute Agreement With Nscale
DataCenterDynamics, reporting Bloomberg
Anthropic has signed a $45 billion AI cloud capacity agreement with the neocloud provider Nscale, according to Bloomberg reporting dated August 26, 2026 and carried by DataCenterDynamics on August 27. The deal covers 460MW leased over six years at Nscale’s Monarch Compute Campus in West Virginia, running NVIDIA Vera Rubin chips expected to come online late in 2027. Nscale acquired the 2,250-acre site in March 2026; it carries potential capacity of up to 8GW, with a first phase of 2GW targeted for the first half of 2028 and power supplied by Caterpillar G3500-series natural gas gensets. Microsoft has separately contracted for 1.35GW at the same campus, and Nscale is preparing a United States IPO reportedly targeting around $3 billion. One sourcing caveat worth stating plainly: this is Bloomberg citing people familiar with the agreement, and DataCenterDynamics notes it has contacted Nscale for confirmation — neither company has confirmed the contract on the record.

Georgia Regulators Approve a 3.2GW Power Contract for a Planned OpenAI Data Center
DataCenterDynamics
The Georgia Public Service Commission has approved a power supply contract between Georgia Power and OpenAI to serve a planned data center campus in Effingham County, Georgia, reported August 27, 2026. Georgia Power will develop 3.2GW of new generation, with OpenAI committing to pay the full cost of the infrastructure required to serve the site; the type of generating capacity has not been disclosed. OpenAI will also provide up to 1GW of flexible demand response, and Georgia Power expects to begin delivering energy between 2028 and 2032. The campus sits within the 2,600-acre Savannah Gateway Industrial Hub, is planned as four buildings totalling 4.4 million square feet, is expected to represent more than $30 billion of OpenAI investment, and uses a closed-loop water cooling system.

Z.ai Releases GLM-5.3 Open Weights, a 753 Billion Parameter Post-Training Update
Hugging Face model card
Z.ai published GLM-5.3 to Hugging Face on August 28, 2026, a 753 billion parameter mixture-of-experts model released under a custom glm-5.3 licence, alongside GLM-5.3-Flash the previous day. Qwen Open-Weights Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, a Preview of the Qwen4 Architecture
Hugging Face model card
Alibaba’s Qwen team released Qwen3.8-Flash-Next on Hugging Face under the qwen-community-1.0 licence, described on the model card as an experimental preview of the architecture that will underpin Qwen4; the repository was last updated August 27, 2026. The language model carries 125 billion parameters with 6 billion activated, plus 51 billion n-gram embedding parameters and 4 billion for multi-token prediction, totalling 180 billion on disk, across 48 layers with 512 experts using top-8 routing plus one shared expert, and a native 262,144-token context extensible to one million via YaRN. Architectural changes include replacing gated attention with Qwen Sparse Attention operating at micro-block level, a gated residual mechanism across four branches, and a 20 million entry bigram and trigram embedding table indexed at layer 2. Reported scores include 62.5 on SWE-bench Pro, 81.0 on SWE-bench Multilingual, 91.7 on GPQA Diamond, 91.9 on LiveCodeBench v6 and 35.9 on Humanity’s Last Exam.

Policy & Regulation
Delaware Enacts Four Bills Making Large Energy Users Supply Their Own Power
WGMD; WHYY; Delaware General Assembly bill text
Delaware Governor Matt Meyer signed four energy bills into law on August 26, 2026, all effective immediately. HB 445 establishes a bring-your-own-generation requirement obliging large energy-use facilities to produce or secure 100 percent of their required power, including a share of clean or renewable energy; the bill’s synopsis requires operators to file a ramp-up plan with the Public Service Commission and reach 100 percent in-state production by their tenth year of operation. HB 233 creates a separate utility rate class for large energy-use facilities, requires them to pay the full cost of transmission, distribution and capacity procurement, and prioritises them for curtailment during grid emergencies unless they supply their own generation. HB 310 bars such facilities from job-creation tax credits and licence-fee reductions, and SB 326 increases oversight of non-mandatory capital spending by for-profit monopoly utilities. Delaware currently hosts roughly 19 data centers, though a 1.2GW campus is proposed by Starwood in New Castle.

Research
TTPO Proposes Label-Free Test-Time Training for Math Reasoning
arXiv
A team led by authors at Zhejiang University posted Test-Time Policy Optimization to arXiv on August 27, 2026, addressing the fact that reinforcement learning and on-policy self-distillation both require ground-truth labels and so cannot be used for test-time training. The paper reports that rollouts disagreeing with a majority-vote pseudo-label are typically wrong regardless of whether the vote itself is correct, and builds an asymmetric objective that distills agreeing rollouts while penalising disagreeing ones with grouped reinforcement learning, adding token-level selection that down-weights already-converged positions and penalises only confident errors. Without labels, the authors report TTPO matching label-supervised self-distillation on five competition-level benchmarks and raising Qwen3-1.7B from 38.0 percent to 45.2 percent under test-time training, with gains of 25.2 to 36.4 percent in non-thinking mode. Code and a project page are listed, and the paper is released under CC BY 4.0.
