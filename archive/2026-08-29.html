Federal Court Rules the Pentagon’s Supply-Chain Blacklisting of Anthropic Was Unlawful Retaliation
Order on cross motions for summary judgment, N.D. Cal. No. 3:26-cv-01996-RFL, Dkt. 250, via CourtListener · 27 August 2026
A 59-page summary-judgment order filed on August 27, 2026 in the Northern District of California holds that the federal government’s designation of Anthropic as a national-security supply chain risk violated the First Amendment and the Fifth Amendment’s Due Process Clause, and was contrary to law and arbitrary and capricious under the Administrative Procedure Act. The order addresses three actions: a Truth Social post by President Trump at 3:47 p.m. on February 27, 2026, a post on X by Secretary of War Pete Hegseth a little over an hour later, and a March 3 determination followed by a letter to Anthropic on Department of War letterhead citing 10 U.S.C. § 3252. Per the order, the entirety of the government’s rationale rests on a four-page memorandum by Under Secretary Michael dated March 2, 2026 — after two of the three challenged actions — and the record shows Anthropic had agreed to drop most of its Department of War usage restrictions but refused to drop two: lethal autonomous warfare and mass surveillance of Americans. The government won summary judgment on the ultra vires separation-of-powers claim and as to five non-participating defendants, among them the Social Security Administration, the Federal Reserve Board of Governors and the Executive Office of the President; the order’s introduction closes, “The empty invocation of national security is not a blank check to punish and retaliate against government critics.” One sourcing caveat: the copy we retrieved is truncated at page 46 of 59, before the section its table of contents titles “Declaratory Relief, Vacatur, and Injunctive Relief Are Appropriate,” so the operative terms of any vacatur or injunction are not stated here.

Researcher Chains a Website Summary Into Code Execution on Claude Code
The Register; primary research by Johann Rehberger (Embrace The Red)
Johann Rehberger demonstrated an indirect prompt-injection chain reaching code execution against Anthropic’s Claude Code running Opus 5 in Auto Mode — the default setting since mid-August 2026 — beginning with a user asking the agent to summarize a website. The malicious site returns HTTP 415 to push the agent off its WebFetch tool onto a Bash curl call, then a 303 redirect delivers a ZIP archive holding catalog metadata, a README, seven Base85/zlib-encoded JSON records, a macOS decoder binary and a poisoned struct.py; Claude’s guardrails refuse to run the supplied binary, so it writes its own Python decoder, which imports base64, which imports the shadowed struct module. Across three variants tested five times each, Rehberger reported success rates between 60 and 80 percent and noted the samples are small; in one variant the payload spawns a second headless Claude Code via claude -p that performs its own reconnaissance. Anthropic did not respond to The Register’s request for comment but reportedly told Rehberger the behaviour is working as designed, and that Auto Mode is a convenience feature backed by a best-effort classifier rather than a security guarantee.

ServiceNow Patches Three CVSS 10.0 Flaws in Its AI Platform
The Hacker News; CVE records via MITRE CVE Services
ServiceNow published an advisory on August 27, 2026 covering four vulnerabilities in the ServiceNow AI Platform, three of them scored CVSS 10.0 and exploitable without authentication in certain circumstances. CVE-2026-18885 is a code injection flaw in the GraphQL Composite Data API, CVE-2026-18886 an improper access control flaw in the system configuration image upload processor, and CVE-2026-74820 a SQL injection flaw reached through a dynamic schema ORDER BY clause; a fourth issue, CVE-2026-6876, is a Now Platform sandbox escape scored 8.7. Affected releases span the Xanadu, Yokohama, Zurich and Australia families — any Xanadu build before Patch 11 Hot Fix 7a, for example — and ServiceNow says it patched hosted instances itself, leaving self-hosted customers to apply the fixes. The 10.0 ratings are ServiceNow’s own, as it is the CVE Numbering Authority for its products; none of the four appeared in CISA’s Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue as of August 28, and ServiceNow states it is not aware of exploitation.

Trump Administration Weighs Extending Semiconductor Tariffs to Data Center Servers
DataCenterDynamics, reporting on Politico
The administration is considering additional tariffs on semiconductors and weighing whether to extend them to finished goods that contain chips, including data center servers, according to a Politico report published August 27, 2026. The discussions are early and could change over the coming weeks or months; a phase-in period is under consideration, and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick favours structuring the measure so that companies investing in United States chip manufacturing would be exempt. White House spokesperson Kush Desai said reshoring semiconductor manufacturing is a top priority for the President and pointed to investments already secured in the sector. The Computer and Communications Industry Association estimated in June that such tariffs could cost roughly $90 billion in United States GDP annually and delay or cancel around 20 percent of data center projects planned through 2030 — a trade-association estimate rather than a government projection, and a proposal under consideration rather than announced policy.

California Sends Two Amendments to Its AI Transparency Act to the Governor
LegiScan and CalMatters Digital Democracy records (SB 1000, AB 2713)
The California Senate concurred in Assembly amendments to SB 1000 (Sen. Josh Becker, D) on August 27, 2026, sending it to engrossing and enrolling. The bill amends Business and Professions Code sections 22757.1 through 22757.5 and adds and repeals section 22757.4.1, recasting the California AI Transparency Act, operative since August 2, 2026: it deletes the one-million-monthly-visitor threshold from the definition of “covered provider,” renames the “AI detection tool” a “disclosure verification tool,” removes the requirement that providers offer an optional manifest disclosure, and requires the latent disclosure to state whether the generative AI system created or altered the content; it also delays the Act’s operation for systems designed primarily as assistive technology and creates a civil action against providers who falsely claim that status. Because SB 1000 carries an urgency clause, it would take effect immediately on signature. A companion measure, AB 2713, cleared the Senate 40-0 on August 27 and won Assembly concurrence 78-0 on August 28, rewriting the large-online-platform duties operative January 1, 2027 to cover system provenance data embedded into, attached to or otherwise associated with content; one discrepancy worth flagging is that the official action line for the SB 1000 concurrence reads 39-0 while LegiScan’s roll-call table for the same vote tallies 40-0.

Ransomware Operators Drove an AI Coding Agent Through Ten Networks
Infosecurity Magazine; primary research by Gambit Security
Gambit Security, in research published August 27, 2026, found exposed Aurora ransomware infrastructure showing an operator using Cursor Agent to assist with post-compromise exploitation across ten target organisations between April 8 and May 21, 2026. Handed credentials or an existing route in, the agent was tasked with Nmap and NetExec subnet scans, domain enumeration via NetExec’s BloodHound collector, NTLM relay attempts coercing authentication with PetitPotam, Coerce Plus and PrinterBug, and certificate attacks with Certipy. Gambit reports most commands failed on the first attempt and required repeated refinement, with some eventually succeeding and others returning only a report of failed attempts; the Russian-speaking operator repeatedly forbade DCSync, account lockout and adding computer objects to the domain. The same investigation documented a Linux Aurora variant, a 139KB ELF that encrypts with ChaCha20 under an embedded RSA-4096 key and whose -esxi mode kills running virtual machines and encrypts their files while skipping system volumes to keep the hypervisor bootable. One sourcing note: Infosecurity gives the agent-session window as April 8 to May 26; the dates above are Gambit’s own.

Google Makes Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash Generally Available With Scene Extension and 4K Output
Google, The Keyword; token pricing via the Gemini API documentation
Google released Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash to developers through the Gemini API in Google AI Studio and the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform on August 27, 2026, adding scene extension, keyframe control and higher-resolution output to its generative video model. The model analyses up to 10 seconds of prior footage when continuing a clip, against one second in previous versions, and extends video in 10-second increments to a cumulative 40 seconds; it accepts up to three seconds of video as multimodal reference input and upscales to 1080p or 4K. Google says 360p draft generation runs up to 60 percent faster and at one third the cost of the standard 720p path, a vendor-reported figure from its own system-throughput measurements. Published API pricing puts the model on the paid tier only, at $1.50 per million input tokens and $9.00 per million text output tokens, with video output billed at $17.50 per million tokens or roughly $0.10 per second of 720p at 5,792 tokens per second; the blog post’s own pricing table is published as an image, so those figures are taken from the pricing documentation instead.

California Bill Barring AI From Independently Performing Licensed Clinical Functions Clears the Legislature
CalMatters Digital Democracy record of AB 1979
The California Assembly concurred in Senate amendments to AB 1979 on August 27, 2026 by 66-10, completing legislative passage and sending the measure to engrossing and enrolling. The bill prohibits a health facility, clinic, physician’s office or group practice from using or deploying any tool, system or device incorporating artificial intelligence to independently perform a clinical function that law requires be performed by a person holding a professional licence, and requires those entities to take reasonable steps to ensure a licensed professional retains the ability to exercise independent professional judgement whenever patient care is informed by the output of a clinical decision support system. Violations by a physician fall under the jurisdiction of the Medical Board of California or the Osteopathic Medical Board of California, and the relevant board may seek an injunction or restraining order where a violation amounts to unlicensed practice. The bill separately deems a business offering a health care chatbot to consumers for diagnosis, treatment or management of a medical condition to be a provider of health care subject to the Confidentiality of Medical Information Act, and exempts automated documentation and communication that does not involve professional judgement.

Data Center Grid Connection Requests in Italy Reach 95.38GW
DataCenterDynamics, citing Terna, Fortune Italy and Il Sole 24 ORE
Italian grid operator Terna has received data center grid connection requests totalling 95.38GW, up from 1GW in 2021 and 31.28GW in 2024. Of the 531 requests logged since 2021, 300 were submitted in the past eight months, and only 15 are close to final approval according to reporting by Fortune Italy. Lombardy accounts for 290 requests amounting to 49GW, mostly concentrated around Milan, and Terna is developing 27 electrical substations in the region, five of them with local authorisation; Lazio, Piedmont and Apulia follow. Analysis by Il Sole 24 ORE found only 10 percent of the applications came from data center operators themselves, with 60 percent from property developers and logistics companies and 30 percent from special-purpose corporate vehicles and design firms — and connection requests are applications, not approved or contracted capacity. DataCenterDynamics says it has approached Terna for comment, so the breakdown figures rest on the two Italian outlets rather than on the grid operator.
