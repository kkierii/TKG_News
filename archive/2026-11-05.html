OpenAI says an unreleased cyber model produced 948 Linux root exploits for $1.2 million
The Hacker News, citing OpenAI · 5 November 2026
OpenAI said on Wednesday that a prototype pipeline running an upcoming cyber model analysed 2,308 candidate Linux kernel findings in a mid-October run, reproduced 1,724 of them within 30 hours and produced 948 working local privilege escalation exploits for a commonly used Linux distribution. The run cost $1,213,430 in API credits, or $1,279 per exploit, and also produced proofs of concept for 291 information leaks. OpenAI says the agents used at least 10 novel exploitation techniques, and that security researchers review each exploit, patch and proposed severity before it is reported to maintainers. The figures are OpenAI’s own.

AI Security
Xen credits Anthropic’s Claude with finding flaws that let one guest VM read and write another’s memory
The Hacker News · 4 November 2026
The Xen Project published advisory XSA-523 on 3 November for CVE-2026-102703 and CVE-2026-102704, which let a malicious guest VM read and write the memory of other VMs on the same host, including dom0. All Xen versions from 4.6 onward are affected; 4.5 and earlier are not. The project credited Anthropic, using its Claude model, with the find, validated by Stephan Lipp of Ada Logics. Patches are out and there is no known mitigation; as of 4 November Debian’s tracker still listed bookworm, trixie and forky as vulnerable.

At least a dozen AI developer platforms breached in a month as WorkOS, Northflank and Trigger.dev disclose hacks
SecurityWeek · 4 November 2026
SecurityWeek counts at least a dozen AI developer platforms hit over the past month, most recently CommandCode, Northflank, Trigger.dev, Chainstack and WorkOS, with attackers hunting for AI access tokens they could resell. Chainstack and WorkOS linked their intrusions to Artex, an open-source AI penetration-testing framework that several companies tie to the Chinese group Storm-0940. Wiz attributes the surge to LLMjacking and LLM broker infrastructure, and found two cases with an identical first unauthorised action within 30 minutes of credential access.

AI industry super PACs spent millions on the midterms and most of their candidates lost
TechCrunch · 4 November 2026
American Mission and Building the Future together spent $4.8 million backing South Carolina Senate candidate Darline Graham, who lost. Guardrails Alliance, which campaigns against AI company money, spent more than $1 million apiece opposing two candidates who lost.

AI Security
Meta has paid about $1.2 million in bug bounties for its Muse AI agent, topped by a $300,000 award
SecurityWeek · 4 November 2026
Meta says its Muse agent bounty, private from April and public since the agent’s 8 September launch, has received more than 630 reports, 64 of them valid. The top $300,000 award went to a pre-launch flaw that exposed conversations and tokens on internal Muse test VMs. Prompt injection affecting a single user can earn up to $130,000, and a fleetwide compromise up to $250,000.

Tencent releases Hy4, its 770B model, adding image understanding and computer use
ChainCatcher · 4 November 2026
Tencent Hunyuan released Hy4 on 4 November, its first model in the series to support visual understanding and computer use, following an August preview. Policy & Regulation
EU court adviser says Gemini answers can infringe press publishers’ rights in some circumstances
IPWatchdog · 4 November 2026
Advocate General Maciej Szpunar of the Court of Justice of the EU issued an opinion on 3 November in a case Hungarian web portal operator Like Company brought against Google over Gemini in the Budapest Regional Court. He said whether a chatbot answer infringes the press publishers’ right depends on the circumstances, and that the right does not subsist in mere facts. On training, he said the EU text and data mining exception can in principle cover the copies made, including for commercial model development. The opinion is not binding on the court.
