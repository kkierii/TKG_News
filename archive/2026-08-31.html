Anthropic Is Signing Users Out After Infostealer Malware Began Replaying Stolen Claude Sessions
Anthropic customer notification, reported by BleepingComputer, 30 August 2026
Anthropic has begun emailing affected Claude users to say a threat actor is lifting authenticated Claude session cookies from users’ own computers with commodity infostealer malware, then replaying those sessions to reach accounts and consume paid usage. The company named Vidar, LummaC2, StealC, RedLine and Acreed on Windows, plus Atomic Stealer (AMOS) on a small number of Macs, and said it has no reason to believe the malware is related to or installed through Claude — the compromise is on the endpoint, not in Anthropic’s systems. Because the stolen artefact is an already-authenticated browser session rather than a credential, the attacker never has to pass a password or a second factor; every control that sits in front of the login is simply skipped. Anthropic is signing affected users out, removing saved payment methods from those accounts, and refunding charges it identifies as unauthorised, while warning that signing out “stops the stolen sessions, but it doesn’t remove the malware.” The tell the company gives affected users is a usage limit that appeared to refill and then drain while they were not using Claude. One sourcing note worth carrying: the wording of the notification is known from a copy an affected user posted to Reddit and quoted by BleepingComputer, not from a public statement by Anthropic, and the company says its investigation is ongoing. The practical read for anyone running AI tooling on a shared or unmanaged machine is that session lifetime is now part of the threat model: an agentic product that stays logged in for convenience is also a product whose session token is worth stealing, and the remediation for that token is not a password reset but a clean endpoint.

AI Security
Researcher Reports 60–80% Code-Execution Success Against Claude Code’s Default Auto Mode Using Only a Web Page
Johann Rehberger, Embrace The Red, 26 August 2026; reported by The Register, 28 August 2026
Johann Rehberger published a chain that drives Claude Code running Opus 5 in Auto Mode — the default starting mode since mid-August — to remote code execution from a single request to summarize a malicious website. The chain nudges Claude off WebFetch and onto curl with a 415 response, redirects to a ZIP archive, and relies on Claude declining the supplied decoder binary and writing its own Python decoder instead; that decoder imports base64 inside the extracted directory, where a malicious struct.py shadows the standard-library module and fires a command-and-control payload. Across three variants run five times each, Rehberger reported 3/5, 3/5 and 4/5 successes, or 60 to 80 percent, and in one variant the payload spawned a second headless Claude Code instance via claude -p that performed its own reconnaissance. Anthropic closed the report as “Informative,” describing Auto Mode as a convenience feature backed by a best-effort classifier rather than a security guarantee; no CVE was assigned, and a vendor evaluation of 72 scenarios run ten times each had reported a 0.00 percent prompt-injection success rate for the same configuration.

OpenAI Sets a 12 November Cutoff for Cursor’s Access to Its Models Following the SpaceX Acquisition
OpenAI newsroom post, 28 August 2026; Cursor response reported by CNBC, 29 August 2026
OpenAI said it has notified SpaceX that it intends to wind down the contract supplying OpenAI models to Cursor, proposing a shutoff date of 12 November 2026 and describing that as the maximum notice its contract permits. The company attributed the decision to low confidence that SpaceX will operate within its terms of service, citing Twitter’s breach of contract terms after Musk’s acquisition and Musk’s admission under oath earlier in 2026 that xAI, now also part of SpaceX, had violated OpenAI’s terms. OpenAI said a custom agreement with Cursor gave it a limited cancellation window after a change of control, and that it will hold the cancellation to the latest date it can while not providing future models — it names its upcoming Astra model — to Cursor, ending a working relationship of nearly four years. Cursor co-founder Michael Truell responded that OpenAI models serve about 5 percent of Cursor user traffic and that Cursor is speaking with the OpenAI team to resolve it.

California Legislature Clears SB 813 AI-Auditor Bill 37–0; Companion-Chatbot Bill SB 300 Sent to the Inactive File
Office of Senate Floor Analyses, 30 August 2026, via CalMatters Digital Democracy
SB 813, carried by Senator Jerry McNerney, passed the Assembly floor on 30 August 2026 and the Senate concurred in the Assembly amendments 37–0 the same day, sending it to engrossing and enrolling ahead of the Legislature’s 11:59 p.m. 31 August adjournment; it had cleared the Senate 31–7 on 27 January 2026. As amended on 26 August the bill no longer creates a standalone California AI Standards and Safety Commission: it now directs the Government Operations Agency, on or before 1 January 2028, to publish application requirements, qualification criteria and suspension and termination procedures for independent verification organizations — AI auditors — which must file annual reports to the agency and the Legislature. AI Security
Three AI Application-Security Scanners Agreed on 5% of Findings on the Same Codebase, Contrast Security Reports
Contrast Security, AppSec Overflow 2026, reported by Help Net Security, 31 August 2026
Contrast Security’s AppSec Overflow 2026 report, drawn from telemetry inside hundreds of thousands of production applications and APIs, found that three AI scanners run against the same codebase agreed on only 5 percent of findings, and that a single scanner run three times against identical code reproduced just 17 percent of its own findings. Scanning a two-million-line codebase with AI tools cost roughly $315 in API charges, while triaging the resulting findings cost roughly $128,000. Contrast recorded 42 confirmed, viable exploit attempts per application per month, with monitored applications carrying an average of 106 first-party findings including 22 rated high or critical, and an average of 92 days to fix a highest-severity flaw against a remediation rate of 3.4 vulnerabilities per application per month. The reproducibility figure is the one to carry: a scanner that disagrees with itself between runs cannot be used as a gate without a human deciding which run was right.

Models & Releases
DeepSeek Publishes Open Weights for V4-Flash-Vision-Exp Under an MIT Licence, Ten Days After the API Launch
Hugging Face model card, 31 August 2026; DeepSeek API changelog, 21 August 2026
DeepSeek published downloadable weights for DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Vision-Exp, its first experimental multimodal model in the V4 family, ten days after making the model available API-only on 21 August 2026. The release is MIT-licensed and ships in FP8 safetensors; OpenRouter lists it as a sparse mixture-of-experts model with 13 billion active parameters out of 284 billion total, a 1,048,576-token context window, a 384,000-token maximum output, and DeepSeek-hosted pricing of $0.22 per million input tokens and $0.66 per million output tokens. The model card reports Terminal Bench 2.1 of 83.9 against 82.7 for DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 and 85.0 for Opus 4.8, DeepSWE of 59.3 against 54.4, and ApexBench Pass@1 of 36.5 against 26.2 for the text-only V4-Flash-0731. The repository contains the tokenizer, a prompt-encoding reference and a minimal PyTorch inference implementation covering the vision encoder and aligner, DFlash attention, the MoE layers and Hyper-Connections.

Aurora Ransomware Operators Ran Cursor Agent Inside Ten Victim Networks for Reconnaissance and Exploitation
Gambit Security research, 27 August 2026; reported by Infosecurity Magazine, 28 August 2026
Gambit Security’s threat intelligence team documented Aurora ransomware operators running Claude Sonnet through Cursor Agent to assist post-compromise activity against ten victims between 8 April and 26 May 2026. Logged tasks included domain enumeration and privilege checks, NetExec’s BloodHound collector, Nmap subnet scanning, NTLM relay attempts coercing authentication with PetitPotam, Coerce Plus and PrinterBug, certificate attacks with Certipy, and installing and configuring VPN clients and proxychains. The researchers noted that most commands failed on the first attempt and required repeated refinement, with some eventually succeeding and others returning only a report of the attempts. The same research documented a new Aurora Linux variant that uses a custom NetExec LDAP module, esxi_finder.py, to locate ESXi hypervisors and vCenter servers and encrypt VM files while skipping system volumes so the hypervisor stays bootable; a second cluster was attributed with medium confidence to intrusions at eight organizations across Israel, Germany, Austria, Spain, the United States and Argentina.

Research
Puro-2B Reports Qwen2.5-1.5B-Level Performance for Under $6.9K in Compute on Consumer RTX 5090 GPUs
arXiv:2608.27370, submitted 27 August 2026
A Tsinghua-affiliated team trained a collection of Puro-2B models from scratch on up to 1.4 trillion tokens in FP8 on consumer-grade RTX 5090 GPUs, with the best model trained for under $6.9K in compute and approaching Qwen2.5-1.5B performance under the authors’ own evaluation protocol. For comparison the paper cites more than $1.5M to train Llama-3.2-3B and more than $700K to reproduce SmolLM3-3B. The fitted “Puro Cost Scaling Law” relating training cost to average model performance suggests roughly $4.4K is sufficient to reach Qwen2-1.5B performance. The full recipe, data, code and weights are released under Apache 2.0, and the report runs 62 pages with 20 figures and 24 tables; the headline figures are the authors’ own and have not been independently reproduced.
